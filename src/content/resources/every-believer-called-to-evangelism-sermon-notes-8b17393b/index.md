Every Believer Is Called to Evangelism<br>
Sermon notes \| Main passage: <span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 28:18–20</span> \| Scripture references: NIV


Central thought: Every disciple of Jesus is commissioned to make disciples, proclaim the gospel, and demonstrate<br>
God's love. Evangelism is not reserved for those with the ministry title of evangelist. The church must move outward, putting Jesus' commands into practice.


1\. The Church Must Turn Outward<br>
A church can become deeply committed to worship, prophetic ministry, fellowship, and discipleship while gradually losing its focus on those who do not know Christ. Spiritual encounters matter, but they should move believers toward the harvest.

• The harvest is plentiful; the need is for laborers who will go (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 9:37–38</span>).

• Evangelism should be an ongoing culture, not an occasional event.

• Pray for, build relationships with, and intentionally share the gospel with people outside the church.


2\. The Great Commission Belongs to Every Disciple
<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 28:18–20</span>. Jesus declares that all authority in heaven and on earth belongs to Him. On that basis He commands His followers to make disciples, baptize them, and teach them to obey everything He commanded.

Discipleship includes moral and devotional obedience, but also Jesus' missional commands. His authority is the foundation for going, rather than waiting until every spiritual obstacle has been identified.

<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 10:7–8</span>. Jesus sends disciples to announce the kingdom and minister healing and deliverance. <span class="scripture-reference">Luke 10:1–9</span> describes a wider group of disciples sent to bring peace, heal the sick, and proclaim the kingdom.

• Learn to obey Jesus' commands before limiting yourself to a particular ministry specialty.

• A disciple is meant to participate in making other disciples.


3\. Gifts Equip the Church; They Do Not Replace Basic Obedience
<span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 4:11–12</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 12:3–8</span>; 1 Corinthians 12. Ministry gifts serve the whole body. Apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors, and teachers equip believers for ministry.

A focus on discovering personal gifts can become unhealthy if it causes people to conclude that witnessing, praying for the sick, or making disciples belongs only to specialists. Evangelists equip the church to evangelize; they do not perform all evangelism on its behalf.

• Healthy equipping restores, strengthens, positions, and releases believers to function.

• The goal is a church that reproduces the ministry and message of Jesus.


4\. The Gospel and the Power of God Belong Together
<span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 3:1–5</span>. The Spirit and God's work among believers are received through faith, not by observing the law. 1
<span class="scripture-reference">Corinthians 1:18</span> connects the message of the cross with God's power.




1


The church cultivates what it continually teaches and practices. Proclaim Christ crucified and risen; make room for prayer, testimony, healing, and Spirit-led ministry. <span class="scripture-reference">John 14:12</span> calls believers to faith expressed in action.

• Do not stop with a healing, prophecy, or spiritual encounter. Explain the gospel and invite a response.

• The miracle serves the person and points toward Jesus; it is not the final goal.


5\. Love Gives Faith Courage to Act
<span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 5:6</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">2 Corinthians 5:14</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">1 John 4:18</span>. Faith expresses itself through love. Christ's love compels believers to reach people, and perfect love drives out fear.

Evangelism cannot be driven by anger, superiority, or the need to protect a reputation. God's love enables believers to see beyond someone's behavior and offer the hope of Christ.

<span class="scripture-reference">Acts 4:29–31</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 6:19–20</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Proverbs 29:25</span>. Even the early apostles asked for boldness. Fear of people becomes a snare, but believers can ask God for courage and words to speak.

• Boldness is cheerful courage: speak clearly, smile, and genuinely love the person in front of you.


6\. Become an Everyday Witness<br>
Everyday places can become opportunities for ministry: workplaces, schools, neighborhoods, public transport, and ordinary conversations. Begin where you are rather than waiting for a platform or formal invitation.

• Write down people you know who do not follow Jesus, and pray for them regularly.

• Build authentic relationships, share your testimony, and invite them to hear the gospel.

• Learn to explain Jesus' death, resurrection, forgiveness, repentance, and lordship simply.

• Be ready to speak to one person as well as a crowd.


7\. Sow, Water, and Reap
<span class="scripture-reference">1 Corinthians 3:6–9</span>. Paul planted, Apollos watered, and God brought growth. Every gospel conversation can contribute to someone's journey, even when there is no immediate decision.

People may hear the gospel multiple times before responding; others may encounter God's power and respond quickly.<br>
The reported survey figures and conversion percentages in the teaching are illustrative claims, not independently verified here.

• Do not judge every encounter only by an immediate conversion.

• Keep sharing faithfully while leaving the growth to God.


8\. Pray for the Sick With Faith and Compassion
<span class="scripture-reference">Luke 10:8–9</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Mark 16:20</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">John 5:19</span>. Jesus' ministry joined proclamation and action. The teaching encourages believers to offer healing prayer, attend to the Holy Spirit's leading, and act in faith even when they feel nothing unusual.

• Ask permission before praying. Pray simply in Jesus' name.

• Pay attention to the person's response; do not exaggerate results.

• Where appropriate, ask whether anything has changed and whether they would welcome further prayer.


2


• A healing testimony should lead into the good news of Jesus, not end with the experience.

<span class="scripture-reference">James 5:16</span>. Confession, humility, and mutual prayer are important in the life of believers. This is not a reason to assume that sickness proves personal sin.


9\. Two Practical Miracle Questions<br>
Question 1: “If God could do anything for you, answer a prayer or do a miracle, what would you want Him to do?”

Listen carefully. When a real need is shared, ask permission to pray. Keep the interaction warm, respectful, and natural.

Question 2: “What do you think is the greatest thing God has already done for you?”

Use the second question to explain the gospel: God sent His Son; Jesus died for our sins and rose again; forgiveness and a new relationship with God are offered through repentance and faith.

A simpler opening is: “Have you ever heard the word gospel? Do you know what it means?” Ask permission to explain it briefly.


10\. Explain the Gospel and Invite a Genuine Response
<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 3:23</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 6:23</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 10:9–10</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">John 3:16</span>. All have sinned. Jesus died for sins and rose from the dead. Salvation is received through faith in Him, repentance, and confessing Him as Lord.

• Sharing a church invitation or personal story is good, but neither replaces explaining the gospel.

• Give people a clear opportunity to respond: “Is there any good reason why you couldn't receive Christ as Savior and<br>
Lord today?”

• Never pressure someone into repeating words they do not mean. A sincere personal response matters more than a formula.

• Help new believers connect with the church and begin following Jesus.


11\. Genuine Welcome Is Part of Our Witness
<span class="scripture-reference">John 13:34–35</span>. The love believers show one another is part of their witness to the world. A friendly greeting is only a beginning; genuine hospitality follows through and builds relationships.

• Notice visitors or people who are standing alone.

• Reach beyond familiar friendship circles.

• Make sure people have an opportunity to hear and respond to the gospel, even during other teaching series.


12\. The Gospel Is Urgent
<span class="scripture-reference">2 Corinthians 6:2</span>. The call to respond to God's salvation is for today. No one knows how much time they have.

The closing testimony concerned a visitor who received Christ after church and died the following day. The urgency of evangelism was summarized in one line:

“The gospel is only good news if it gets there on time.”

Love people enough to speak, pray, invite, and follow through. Every believer can begin now.
