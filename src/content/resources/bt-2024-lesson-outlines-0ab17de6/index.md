Session 6: Church Government (Chapter 13, pp. 15-23)

I. Everything Needs a Head<br>
II. God is the Head over All<br>
a. Appoints delegated authority in His Church<br>
b. Establishes authority in the earth – <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 13:1</span><br>
c. Biblical pictures of delegated authority: patriarchs, elders of nations<br>
III. God’s Delegated Authority<br>
a. Not chosen by democracy, but appointed by God<br>
b. Men must be under authority – <span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 8:5-9</span><br>
c. Men must recognize those who are appointed – <span class="scripture-reference">Numbers 12:1-15</span><br>
IV. NT Leaders – Definitions<br>
a. Elder/Biship/Overseer/Pastor/Shepherd – indicates function, not an office.<br>
i. Responsible to watch over, feed, and guard the flock/culture of the church.<br>
ii. <span class="scripture-reference">1 Timothy 4:13</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Titus 1:5-7</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 5:2</span> b. Deacons – servant ministers i. Serves the church body.<br>
ii. NT pictures of deacons: Stephen & Philip – anointed, spirit-filled, miracles iii. Same qualifications as elders (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Timothy 3:8</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Philippians 1:1</span>)<br>
V. Church Government works in Team<br>
a. Each team has a senior leader.<br>
b. Biblical leadership teams are not configurations of colleagues or staff; they are submitted to one another.<br>
c. Examples: <span class="scripture-reference">Acts 14:23, 15</span>:22, 20:27, <span class="scripture-reference">1 Timothy 5:17</span><br>
VI. Five-fold Ministry Offices –<br>
a. Apostles, Prophets, Evangelist, Pastors, Teachers – given to equip the saints and bring the church to maturity – <span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>) i. Apostles – establish, strengthens, and fathers churches/leaders.<br>
ii. Prophets – release revelation; lays foundations in church; builds leaders iii. Evangelist – proclaims Gospel to win souls; train and equips believers too iv. Pastor – cares for and feeds the sheep; not called to a be a one-man show v. Teacher – skilled and anointed to unfold revelation in the Word of God b. Every elder in a church should have a five-fold calling, but those with five-fold callings are not always church elders.<br>
VII. Jesus is All in All<br>
a. Jesus is the ultimate fulfillment of Five-fold ministry.<br>
b. Recognize God-appointed leaders and their grace to build God’s people/church<br>
VIII. Other Gifts to the Church<br>
a. Administration, giving, helps, hospitality, intercession, psalmists, and so forth.<br>
b. People operate in submitted graces when not in direct leadership, but under leadership<br>
IX. Conclusion: The Church belongs to God—it is His family/body, His government, His<br>
People, His Gifts, His callings, and His Leaders.

Session 9: Praise, Worship, Prayer, & Prophetic (Chapter 16, pp. 39-45 & Chapter 18, pp. 53-58)

I. Praise and Worship.<br>
a. Praise is the joyful response of all creatures to God’s greatness (<span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 150:6</span>).<br>
b. Worship is the tender and intimate response of God’s children to His fatherhood and majesty (<span class="scripture-reference">John 4:23</span>) c. Worship belongs to God and includes a myriad of expressions, such as lifting our hands, clapping, shouting/dancing, playing instruments, & singing (Ps. 150)<br>
II. Prayer<br>
a. Prayer is intentional communication with God, which deepens intimacy and fosters relationship with Him.<br>
b. Prayer is dynamic and includes various aspects such as confession, worship, meditation, petition, thanksgiving, intercession, praying in the Spirit, and can be coupled with fasting (see pages 55-56 for verse references).<br>
c. Prayer should be engaged without ceasing, thus it can be spontaneous or scheduled and regardless of structure, it must be sincere (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Thessalonians 5:17</span>).<br>
III. Prophetic<br>
a. Being prophetic is the faith-filled response and nature of following the Spirit of<br>
God as He leads—as a local church, corporate family, and individual believers, this looks like seeking God specifically for His will, ways, and word in any and every situation, which when discerned, heard, and obeyed releases kingdom manifestations and impact. (<span class="scripture-reference">2 Corinthians 5:7</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 8:14</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 5:18</span>).<br>
b. As prophetic people we are willing to release, receive, weigh, and judge prophetic words; we make space for honorable prophetic ministry that draws people closer to Jesus (e.g., prophecy, prophetic singing, prophetic exhortation, use of prophetic gifts tongues/interpretation, words of knowledge/wisdom, prayers of deliverance/breakthrough) (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 10:31</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">1 Corinthians 12:7-11</span>).<br>
c. As prophetic people we honor and receive authentic 5-fold ministry gifts of apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors, and teachers, recognizing their grace to strengthen local churches and edify believers. (<span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 5:11-13</span>)
