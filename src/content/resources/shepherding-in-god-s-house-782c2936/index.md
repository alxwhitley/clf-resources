## Shepherding God's House 2


Introduction<br>
Any call to leadership in the house of God has an element of shepherding. Why? Because both<br>
God and Jesus identify themselves that way (<span class="scripture-reference">Psalms 23:1-6</span>, "The Lord is my Shepherd," John<br>
10:1-18, "I am the Good Shepherd).

Over the past 50-60 years, the church growth movement, while bringing some positive changes to the body of Christ, has also had its drawbacks, particularly in leadership. It has fostered a leadership culture that often mirrors the corporate world, with a CEO, megachurch, and mega leader mentality, which is a stark contrast to the shepherding model.

God has ordained leadership in His House to resemble a family of shepherds, mirroring the way natural shepherds operated in the Biblical world. These shepherds, like moms and dads, nurture and guide His family flock. The Bible contains about 702 references to shepherds and sheep, many of which outline God’s design for leadership among His people. Moreover, the New<br>
Testament employs family imagery to depict God and His people a staggering 1377 times. In contrast, contemporary leadership concepts often focus on “Ministers” who oversee meetings, programs, and organizations, rather than shepherds who care for God's family like parents.

Regardless of our specific roles, whether we are apostles, prophets, pastors, teachers, evangelists, school teachers, daycare workers, parents, or siblings, we all share a common thread: the shepherding aspect. This underscores the need to cultivate God's shepherding heart as we serve His people.

1\. Admonish the unruly: The Greek word “admonish (noutheteo)” means “to warn, caution, to urge to duty, or to remind of obligation.” The word for the “unruly (Ataktos)” means “the disorderly, out of rank, or deviation from the prescribed order.”

The word ataktos was often used in the military sense. When used in a military sense, it had the idea of a soldier out of line, a soldier out of rank, a soldier who was guilty of disorderly conduct, who was insubordinate, non-submissive, disobeying orders, or not following through on his duty. He was out of step. It eventually came to mean anybody who does not do his duty or anybody who does not follow through on his responsibility. Moffit translates it as "loafers".<br>
Some have suggested it as the quitters, idle, lazy, indolent, or apathetic. But it does not have to mean just that. It can mean someone who does not do his duty, not only out of apathy but someone who does not do his duty out of rebellion.

In a current sense, it refers to the wayward. They are out of line. They are out of step. Everybody else is going in one direction; they are not. Everybody else understands spiritual duty and is willing to do it. People will do whatever God has gifted them to do. They tend to be behind the church's leadership, supporting their direction. They are on board, on the team, participating, and involved. These are the kind of people that make the church move and grow.

The unruly reject all that. They are out of step. It may be that they are not doing their duty because they do not care; it may be that they are not doing their duty because they are angry, rebellious, or contentious. They are just not supportive. They are not a part of what is going on.<br>

## Calling, Caring, and Consistent Reminding

The Holy Spirit spoke a word in my heart while I was leading a study time with a group of students. It is not what you say to them that they will remember, but it is what you continually say to them that they will remember."
