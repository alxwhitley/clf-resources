The life of Joseph is a phenomenal testament to the grace of God to forgive under the most severe wounding, betrayal, and injustice.<br>
Joseph family of origin, depicted in the book of Genesis, is a microcosm of wounding, hurt, and pain.<br>
Jacob, Joseph’s father, was a twin who was wounded by FAVORITISM of his parents (25:28); unfortunately the generational effects of this passed through him and into his marriage (29:30-31) and parenting (37:3)…although the God eventually delivered him and healed his identity (32:24-30).<br>
Nearly every person in the Joseph's family suffered parental wounding:<br>
His mother and stepmother/aunt, Rachel and Leah, were both wounded by their father Laban (31:19) and found themselves in fierce competition for the love and affection of the same man (30:14-16); their relationship was rife with envy (30:1) and wrestling (30:8).<br>
Joseph’s brothers and sister were caught in the crossfires of competition, manipulation, and favoritism and received deep parental wounds that affected them for the majority of their adult lives, producing a multitude of sinful responses:<br>
Rueben – disrespected his father and promiscuous (35:22), eventually cursed as “less than” and bound to defeat (49:3-4)<br>

Dinah – insecure, vulnerable, unwise in search for connection/friendship (34:1-2)<br>
Joseph/Benjamin – wounded by the death of their mother (35:6-19), the manipulation/favoritism of their father (37:2,14; 42:2; 44:20), and hatred of their brothers.<br>
Joseph is one of the few characters in the Bible that we do not have a clear record of personal sin in his story \[although we often infer that he was prideful, arrogant (37:2, 5, 9, and acquiesced to the Egyptian cultural practice of divination (44:15)\]. Joseph suffered significant wounding and had a number of hurtful/harmful deeds sown into his life that could have sprung up as seeds of bitterness, defiling him and causing Him (and others) to miss out on fulfilling his purpose and assignment.<br>

Jacob, his father – favoritism, manipulation<br>

His brothers – hatred, mistreatment, jealousy/envy, strife, abuse<br>
God (in his own mind) – giving him dreams and failing to fulfill them (possibly determined/felt in the midst of his trials)<br>
Potipher/Mrs. Potipher – forced enslavement, temptation and false accusation<br>
Prison mates / Pharoah’s servants – forgetting, abandoning him in prison for an extended time<br>
Despite all of these opportunities to remain hurt, offended, or be defiled—Joseph overcame by the grace of God, was healed of His hurts, transformed through his pain such that his prophetic gift/grace/administration was pressed into service without insecurity, malice, or self-preservation; and recognized the overarching good and merciful intentions of God in his horrific situation:
