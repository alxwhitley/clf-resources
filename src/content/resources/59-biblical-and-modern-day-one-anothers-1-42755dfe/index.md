The 59 “One Anothers” of the NT<br>
Jesus isn't building an audience of spectators for a Sunday Morning service. He is building a family of disciples, His church, to engage in His mission to the earth. He didn't create us to be watching stones but living stones contributing to the process of being built up as His house/family (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Pet 2:1-5</span>).

• <span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 2:19, 4</span>:16 MKJV in Whom you are being built together for a dwelling place of God through the<br>
Spirit… from whom the whole body, fitted together and compacted by that which every joint supplies, according to the effectual working in the measure of each part produces the growth of the body…

All of God’s people are created for “One Another” Life.

The calling on God’s people is to live interdependent, not independent lives. The New Testament’s imageries that describe the corporate nature of God’s people are His family, the body of Christ, the temple of God, and flocks of sheep (sheepfolds). This interdependent life God has called us to live together is seen in the use of the word “One Another” (Gk Allelon). It is a plural pronoun that means<br>
“one another, reciprocally, together, and mutually.” It occurs 100 times in 94 verses in the New<br>
Testament. Of those verses, 59 contain direct commands that we are to practice. Gordon Fee expressed it well, “Our interdependence is particularly seen in Paul's relentless use of the Greek word allelon--"each other." Everything is done "allelon.”




Practicing the “One Another’s” frees us from one of the biggest problem with the corruptive nature of sin, “Self-addiction” – Doug<br>
Kreighbaum

Modern Day One Another’s<br>
• Put a spiritually encouraging post, text, or message to someone to stimulate their faith.<br>
• Pray, hear from God, and encourage one another with what God is saying.<br>
• Bless someone by telling them the encouraging perspective of how God has made them.<br>
• Tell someone you love them, write an encouraging card or message.<br>
• Facebook, text, email one another something encouraging (a God blessing).<br>
• Look for ways to praise/compliment someone in front of others.<br>
• Do something special for one person on a regular basis<br>
• Ask how they are doing and provide a listening ear. (this is a great way to bear burdens)<br>
• Pray with them on the spot.


• Open up about a struggle you have that you sense or know they are having.<br>
• Don’t hold a grudge.<br>
• Invite people over or out for a meal, coffee etc.<br>
• Be flexible and put other’s needs above your own (<span class="scripture-reference">Phil 2:3-4</span>)<br>
• Go to a Nursing Home and visit those that have no family.<br>
• Offer to return someone else’s shopping cart to the store from the parking lot.<br>
• Let someone go before you in the grocery checkout line.<br>
• Give an anonymous love offering to someone Offer to babysit for a night out, help finance it.<br>
• Drop in unannounced to encourage someone, bring something with you to bless them, gift, snack etc.<br>
• Cook a special mean for one another.<br>
• Slip envelopes full of money under one another's doors.<br>
• Leave bags of food for one another.<br>
• Do some aspect of one another's housework.<br>
• Insist on washing up for one another.<br>
• Offer rides to one another even if it means going out of your way.<br>
• Share possessions with one another.<br>
• Lend your cars to one another.<br>
• House sit for one another.<br>
• Clean one another’s car.<br>
• Try a new activity with one another simply because the other one likes it.<br>
• Make friends with one another's friends.<br>
• Go out for dinner with one another.<br>
• Go shopping with one another.<br>
• Take the kids to the park with one another.<br>
• Throw surprise parties for one another.
