Relational compass

The enemy, the nature of sin, and our emotional makeup confuse and disorient relationships. If not handled properly sin will break them up.

Camping in Colorado - Hiking and going off the trail causes me to get lost. Nothing is more disorientating and unnerving than to get lost in the wilderness. In those times one of the most important things to have is a compass. The dictionary tells us that a compass measures directions in a frame of reference that is stationary relative to the surface of the earth. A stationary frame of reference is important especially in the challenges of being disorientated when lost.

I have heard it said, and have proven it in my own experience, that more than 90% of church problems are more relational based than any other issue. Even in theological squabbles when you look deeper you see they had a relational foundation. Usually someone was offended somewhere along the way and didn't get it resolved. When a theological disagreement came up there was trauma and separation all rooted in an underlying relational issue.

God's Spirit dealing with us through God's word is our relational compass

Percentages in the Bible - This is why 44% of the NT Epistles are instruction about how to walk in relationships and how to resolve relational issues. The Last Supper 43% of instruction was how to walk with God and 32% was how to walk with each other, 19% was His departure and our mission to the world. .

The critical points of God's relational compass revealed in the Bible

1\. Talk to God before and more than talking to people. Our first instinct in any relational strain should be to go to God in prayer. If we would spend more time talking to Jesus about someone we have problems with than we do talking to others there would be a lot less relational strain that leads to separation, and much more peace.

Php 4:6-7 NASB Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God. 7 And the peace of God, which surpasses all comprehension, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.

1Ti 2:1 First of all, then, I urge that entreaties and prayers, petitions and thanksgivings, be made on behalf of all men,

Going to God in prayer gives us spiritual strength and perspective to enable us to walk through relational strain in a Godly way.

You always vent vertically first. David did this in psalms. In His presence our emotions and our heart becomes tender and we gain a right perspective. We often find that our attitude, more than what anyone else did, is the bigger problem for us. Most frustration with people is really more about perceived and unmet needs, or sins of omission or commission. In prayer we come back to God as our source, first.

2\. Refuse to perpetuate and listen to slander, gossip, or complaining about others

How do you know if it is sharing needed information or slander and gossip? Remember that the essence of Biblical gossip and slander is a sharing of concerns or problems with others with someone who is not part of the problem or solution.

Slander/Backbiting/Evil speaking - defamation, backbiting, evil speaking. To attack the name or reputation of someone. Complaining about people, bring dishonor upon people. To slander an absent person

Gossip/Whisperings/Tale bearing - secret slandering, talk or spread rumors about someone or something other than the person you are talking to.

Rest of the Story “Boxer Rebellion” in China

“In 1899 four reporters from Denver Co. met by chance on a Saturday night at a Denver railroad station. Al Stephens Jack Tournea John Lewis and Hal Wilshire worked for the four Denver papers. The Post the Times the Republican, and the Rockey Mountain news. Each had the unenvious task of finding a scoop for the Sunday Edition. They hoped to spot a visiting celebrity arriving that evening by train. However no one showed up so the reporters wondered what on earth they would do. As they discussed options at a nearby saloon Al suggested that they make up a story. The other three laughed at first but before long they all agreed. They would come up with such a whopper that no one would question it and their respective editors would congratulate them on their find. A local story would be too obvious so they decided to write about someplace far away. They agreed upon China. What if we say that some American engineers on their way to China told us they are bidding on a major job? The Chinese government is planning on demolishing the great wall. Harold was not sure the story would be believable. “Why would the Chinese ever tear down the great wall of China?” “As a sign of international good will and to invite foreign trade.” By 11:00 pm the four reporters had worked out the details and the next day all four Denver newspapers carried the story on the front page. The Times headline that Sunday read "Great Chinese Wall Doomed, Peking seeks world trade" Of course the story was ridiculous. A tall tale made up of four opportunistic newsmen in a hotel bar. But amazingly their story was taken seriously and ran in newspapers in the eastern US and even abroad. When the citizens of China heard that the Americans were sending a demolition crew to dismantle the great wall most were indignant even enraged. Particularly angry were members of a secret society made up of Chinese patriots already against any kind of foreign intervention. Moved to action by the news story they attacked the foreign embassy in Peking and murdered hundreds of Missionaries from abroad. In the next two months 12,000 troops from six countries working together invaded China to protect their countrymen. The bloodshed of that time born out of a journalistic hoax fabricated at a saloon in Denver was a time of violence known every sense as the Boxer rebellion. What power the written or spoken word has, the tiny tongue is a mighty force indeed.”

We are all responsible to preserve the unity of the church by dealing with slander and gossip.

I once heard a great story from Jim Cymbala, pastor of Brooklyn Tabernacle about confronting rumormongers. About 20 years ago, I said something impromptu to the new members lined up across the front of the church. As we received them, the Holy Spirit prompted me to add, "And now, I charge you that if you ever hear another member speak an unkind word of criticism or slander against anyone—myself, an usher, a choir member, or anyone else—that you stop that person in mid-sentence and say, 'Excuse me—who hurt you? Who ignored you? Who slighted you? Was it Pastor Cymbala? Let's go to his office right now. He'll apologize to you, and then we'll pray together so God can restore peace to this body. But we won't let you talk critically about people who aren't present to defend themselves. I'm serious about this. I want you to help resolve this kind of thing immediately. And know this: If you are ever the one doing the loose talking, we'll confront you." To this day, every time we receive new members, I say much the same thing. That's because I know what most easily destroys churches. It's not crack cocaine, government oppression, or even lack of funds. Rather it's gossip and slander that grieves the Holy Spirit.

When hearing information we need to take the do I need to know this test?

Am I part of the problem and therefore can be part of the solution?

Tip “If people don’t want to take active steps to resolve the situation they are probably just venting or complaining, or they don’t want you to examine their hearts it is probably gossip”

3\. Always take the initiative in relational challenges.

Don’t procrastinate, or make excuses. In conflict time heals nothing, it only causes hurts to fester.
