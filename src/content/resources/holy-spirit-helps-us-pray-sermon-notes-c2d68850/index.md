The Holy Spirit Helps Us Pray<br>
Main Scripture: <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 8:26–27</span>

Central Thought: The Holy Spirit helps us pray when we do not know what to pray, how to pray, or what God’s will is. He brings our prayers into alignment with God’s purposes.


1\. Prayer Must Be a Priority in the Church
<span class="scripture-reference">2 Chronicles 7:14</span> — God calls His people to humble themselves, pray, seek His face, and turn from wickedness.
Prayerlessness is a major weakness among believers.

Prayer invites God’s involvement in our families, bodies, and circumstances. The church must value gathering together for prayer. We learn to pray by praying.


2\. The Holy Spirit Helps Us in Our Weakness
<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 8:26–27</span> — “The Spirit helps us in our weakness...” (NIV)

We do not always know what we ought to pray. The Spirit knows our hearts, understands the will of God, and intercedes for us. Just as a lawyer knows how to respond appropriately to a legal matter, the Holy Spirit knows how to intercede when our understanding is limited.


3\. The Holy Spirit Intercedes Beyond Our Words
<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 8:26</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">1 Samuel 1:10–17</span> — Hannah prayed so deeply that her lips moved without an audible voice. Eli misunderstood her, but she was pouring out her soul to God. Samuel was later born in answer to her prayer.

The deepest prayers are not necessarily the loudest or most articulate. God understands the heart when words are insufficient.


4\. The Holy Spirit Connects Our Needs With God’s Supply
<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 8:27</span> — The Spirit knows our real needs and intercedes according to God’s will. We may know that something is missing without knowing the right answer.

Like a knowledgeable employee who knows where to find an item in a large store, the Holy Spirit knows where the answer lies. We have two intercessors: Jesus Christ intercedes at the Father’s right hand (<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 8:34</span>), and the<br>
Holy Spirit intercedes for us.


5\. Prayer Aligns Us With the Will of God
<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 8:27</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Job 42:5–6, 10</span> — Prayer is not about forcing God to agree with us. It brings us into agreement with
Him.

Job wanted answers concerning his suffering. After encountering God, his perspective changed. God told him to pray for his friends, and his fortunes were restored after he did. Sometimes the answer begins when we respond to what God wants rather than focusing only on what we want.


6\. We Can Pray With Confidence
<span class="scripture-reference">John 11:41–42</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">1 John 5:14–15</span> — Jesus thanked the Father at Lazarus’s tomb, confident that the Father heard
Him. John teaches: “If we ask anything according to his will, he hears us.” (<span class="scripture-reference">1 John 5:14</span>, NIV)


1


Confidence in prayer grows from alignment with God’s will. The Holy Spirit helps us pray according to that will, so we can trust that God hears us.


7\. The Holy Spirit Is With Us Wherever We Pray
<span class="scripture-reference">John 14:15–17</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 11:39–40</span> — Jesus promised the Spirit of truth would remain with His followers and dwell in them.

Prayer is not limited to a building, location, time, or posture. We can pray at home, in the car, standing, walking, sitting, or kneeling. Closing our eyes can help concentration, but it is not a requirement.


8\. Praying in Tongues Goes Beyond Our Understanding
<span class="scripture-reference">1 Corinthians 14:2, 14</span> — Paul teaches that a person speaking in a tongue speaks to God, expressing mysteries by the Spirit. When he prays in a tongue, his spirit prays though his mind does not understand.

Our vocabulary, knowledge, memory, and understanding are limited. Praying in tongues is directed toward God, not toward the people listening. The one who needs to understand is God.


9\. Pray With the Spirit and With Understanding
<span class="scripture-reference">1 Corinthians 14:15</span> — Paul teaches both praying with the spirit and praying with understanding, and both singing with the spirit and singing with understanding.

Praying in the Spirit does not replace specific prayers in our own language. Both belong in the believer’s prayer life.


10\. Develop a Consistent Life of Prayer<br>
Prayer is learned through practice. Depend on the Holy Spirit, surrender personal desires to God’s will, pray with understanding and in the Spirit, and build endurance through regular prayer alone and with the church.


Conclusion<br>
The Holy Spirit has been given to help us in prayer. He knows our hearts and the Father’s will, and He helps us pray beyond our natural limitations. Prayer is not about getting God to agree with us. It is about coming into agreement with Him. We learn to pray by praying.

Scripture references use the NIV. Short excerpts are quoted; other passages are summarized.
