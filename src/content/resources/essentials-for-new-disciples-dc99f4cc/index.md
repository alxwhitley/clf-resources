## Essentials For New Disciples 2


The Word of God<br>
"Therefore everyone who hears these words of mine and puts them into practice is like a wise man who built his house on the rock. The rain came down, the streams rose, and the wind blews and beat against that house; yet it did not fall, because it had its foundation on the rock" <span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 7:24-25</span> (read also verse 26-27).<br>
Just like a house, our lives need a strong foundation. The world around us changes, but<br>
God's word - the Bible - never changes (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 1:24-25</span>). It is the absolute foundation for our lives.<br>
"Then we will no longer be infants, tossed back and forth by the waves, and blown here and there by every wind of teaching...Instead...we will in all things grow up into him who is the Head, that is, Christ" <span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 4:14-15</span>.<br>
The Creative Word<br>
God says:"...so is my word that goes out from my mouth: It will not return to me empty, but will accomplish what I desire and achieve the purpose for which I sent it" <span class="scripture-reference">Isaiah 55:11</span> (read also verse 10).<br>
The word of God has great creative power. God created the universe by His word (Psalm<br>
33:6,9; <span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 11:3</span>) and sustains the universe by His word (<span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 1:3</span>). The word of<br>
God has the same kind of creative power in our lives.<br>
The word of God is like water<br>
The word of God cleanses us (Eph.5:25-27). We start life in the Kingdom of God totally<br>
"washed clean" by the Spirit of God through His word (<span class="scripture-reference">John 15:3</span>). And as we continue to read and absorb God's word, it enables us to live a life that pleases God.<br>
"Sanctify them by the Truth; your word is truth" <span class="scripture-reference">John 17:17</span>.<br>
The word of God is like light<br>
Before we knew the Lord, our lives were in spiritual darkness. Now, as we read the Bible, God's word is like a bright light, shining on areas of our lives that God wants to change, and shedding light on the path God wants us to walk with Him.<br>
"Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light for my path" <span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 119:105</span> (read also verse 130; 19:8; <span class="scripture-reference">2 Peter 1:3-9</span>).<br>
The word of God is like seed<br>
"For you have been born again, not of perishable seed, but of imperishable, through the living and enduring word of God" 1 Pet.1:23.<br>
Read the Parable of the Sower in <span class="scripture-reference">Luke 8:4-15</span>. In verse 11, Jesus said: "The seed is the word of God." When we hear God's word, it is like a seed sown into our hearts, designed to bring forth life. If our hearts are hard and unreceptive, the seed is "snatched away." But

if we receive it and obey it - allow it to "take root" - then God's word begins to work in our lives.


## Essentials For New Disciples 3


"...humbly accept the word planted in you, which can save you. Do not merely listen to the word and so deceive yourselves. Do what it says" <span class="scripture-reference">James 1:21</span>- 22.<br>
The word of God is like food<br>
"...Man does not live on bread alone, but on every word that comes from the mouth of God" <span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 4:4</span>.<br>
The word of God causes us to grow spiritually. When we received Christ, we were "born of the Spirit" by the word of God (<span class="scripture-reference">John 3:3,6</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 1:3,23</span>). For us to grow in the<br>
Spirit, we need to continue to feed on God's word.<br>
The word of God is like sword<br>
"For the word of God is living and active. Sharper than any double-edged sword, it penetrates even to the dividing soul and spirit, joints and marrow; it judges the thoughts and attitudes of the heart" <span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 4:12</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 6:17</span>).<br>
The word of God can cut right to our hearts, making us aware of sin (<span class="scripture-reference">Acts 2:36-37</span>).<br>
God's word, stored in our hearts, can also be used as a weapon against the attacks of<br>
Satan (<span class="scripture-reference">Luke 4:1-14</span>).<br>
A Love for God's Word<br>
"Like newborn babies, crave pure spiritual milk, so that by it you may grow up in your salvation, now that you have tasted that the Lord is good" <span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 2:2-3</span> (see also 1 Cor.3:1-2; <span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 19:8-10</span>; 119:103).<br>
It is important to receive God's word regularly, so that we can grow in the Lord. We do this in three ways:<br>
• We sit under regular teaching (<span class="scripture-reference">Acts 2:42</span>; 2 Tim.3:16).<br>
• We read God's word each day (<span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 119:9,11</span>).<br>
• We share God's word with each other (<span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 3:16</span>).<br>
As we absorb God's word, we become familiar with God's will and God's ways. This gives us special authority in prayer (<span class="scripture-reference">John 15:7</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">1 John 5:14-15</span>). Through the word of<br>
God, we discover God's nature, and the Holy Spirit works in our lives to change us into the likeness of Jesus (<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 8:29</span>).<br>
"until we all reach unity in the faith and in the knowledge of the Son of God and become mature, attaining to the whole measure of the fullness of Christ"
<span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 4:13</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 1:25,28</span>).
This is God's plan for our lives - that we will grow up into the full likeness of Jesus<br>
Himself! God does this by changing the attitude of our mind, so that we see things the way God sees them (<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 12:2</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 4:21-24</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 3:9-10</span>).<br>
"he has given us his very great and precious promises, so that through them you may participate in the divine nature" <span class="scripture-reference">2 Peter 1:4</span>.<br>
As we respond in faith to God's "very great and precious promises," His Spirit works each day to change us into the image of Christ. This is the power of God's word in our lives.<br>
"...you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free" <span class="scripture-reference">John 8:32</span>.



## Essentials For New Disciples 4


The Word Become Flesh<br>
"The Word became flesh..." <span class="scripture-reference">John 1:14</span>.<br>
Jesus is called "the Word of God" (<span class="scripture-reference">John 1:1</span>). When He "became flesh" (verse 14), God's message took on human form in the life of Jesus. Now God wants the same thing to happen in your life. God doesn't want you to just read the Bible and learn about Him. He wants His words to become a part of your life - to change you. Then people will not just hear God's word in word form. Like Jesus, they will see the word of God as a daily reality in your life.<br>
"That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked at and our hands have touched - this we proclaim concerning the Word of Life" <span class="scripture-reference">1 John 1:1</span>.


God<br>
"You alone are the Lord. You made the heavens, even the highest heavens, and all their starry host, the earth and all that is on it, the seas and all that is in them. You give life to everything, and the multitudes of heaven worship you" <span class="scripture-reference">Nehemiah 9:6</span>.<br>
Everything in the universe was created by God's hand (<span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 1:16</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 11:3</span>).<br>
This is not just the stars, the sun and the earth. This also includes you.<br>
"For you created my inmost being; you knit me together in my mother's womb. I praise you because I am fearfully and wonderfully made." <span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 139:13-14</span>.<br>
God is far too great for us to fully understand. He has no beginning and He has no end.<br>
But what is God like? The Bible reveals that God is:<br>
All-powerful<br>
"Ah, Sovereign Lord, you have made the heavens and the earth by your great power and outstretched arm. Nothing is too hard for you" <span class="scripture-reference">Jeremiah 32:17</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 19:26</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 3:20</span>).<br>
God's power is without limit. But even more than this, God has the right to use that power in any way He sees fit. This is what the Bible means when it says God is sovereign<br>
(Isa.40:10-29).<br>
Everywhere<br>
"You hem me in - behind and before.Where can I go from your Spirit? Where can<br>
I flee from your presence?" <span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 139:5,7</span> (read also verses 8-10).<br>
God is everywhere. His presence is not just limited to a church or cathedral. He is with you at home, at work, wherever you are.<br>
All-knowing<br>
"O Lord, you have searched me and you know me. You know when I sit and when I rise; you perceive my thoughts from afar. You discern my going out and my lying down; you are familiar with all my ways. Before a word is on my tongue

you know it completely, O Lord" <span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 139:1-4</span> (read the whole Psalm; also 1
<span class="scripture-reference">John 3:20</span>).
God knows everything. His knowledge, like His power, is without limit.<br>
"Nothing in all creation is hidden from God's sight. Everything is uncovered and laid bare before the eyes of him to whom we must give account" <span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 4:13</span>.<br>
God is absolute in knowledge, absolute in power and absolute in presence. But if God knows everything, then He knows every sin I have committed. If He is everywhere, there is no place I can escape His hand. If He is all-powerful, He can judge and destroy me. If all we know is the attributes of God, and know nothing of His nature, we have every reason to fear Him.<br>
".our God is a consuming fire" <span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 12:29</span>.<br>
The Nature of God<br>
The Bible reveals two special parts to God's character:<br>
God is holy<br>
"There is no one holy like the Lord; there is no one besides you." <span class="scripture-reference">1 Samuel 2:2</span><br>
(read also <span class="scripture-reference">Isaiah 6:1-5</span>).<br>
God is absolutely holy. This does not just mean that God cannot do evil. It also means that nothing evil is allowed to enter into God's holy presence (<span class="scripture-reference">Exodus 33:20</span>).<br>
God is love<br>
"For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life" <span class="scripture-reference">John 3:16</span> (see also 1 John<br>
4:10).<br>
God is not only perfect in holiness; He is also perfect in love. He is moved by compassion for His creation (<span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 145:17-18</span>). He is a Person we can get to know<br>
(<span class="scripture-reference">James 4:8</span>).<br>
"And so we know and rely on the love God has for us. God is love." <span class="scripture-reference">1 John 4:16</span>.<br>
A Balanced Nature<br>
"And (God) passed in front of Moses, proclaiming, 'The Lord, the Lord, the compassionate and gracious God, slow to anger, abounding in love and faithfulness, maintaining love to thousands, and forgiving wickedness, rebellion and sin. Yet he does not leave the guilty unpunished; he punishes the children and their children for the sin of the fathers to the third and fourth generation'" Exodus<br>
34:6-7.<br>
It is important to see God's whole nature, not just focus exclusively on one part. God is a<br>
God of love, but He is also a God of holiness. He is a God of compassion, but He is also a<br>
God of justice (read <span class="scripture-reference">Jeremiah 9:23-24</span>). When mankind sinned, God was faced with a problem. He loved us, but His holiness demanded judgment be sentenced against us. He could not turn a blind eye to our sin. So in His wisdom, God sent Jesus to die in our place. Instead of punishing us, as He has the right to, God took on Himself the punishment His holiness demanded. The Cross was where the love of God and the holiness of God met.


## Essentials For New Disciples 6


"But God demonstrates his own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us" <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 5:8</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">1 John 3:1</span>).<br>
Seeing the Unseen God<br>
God is unseen (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Timothy 1:17</span>), but He has revealed Himself in two special ways to mankind:<br>
In His creation<br>
"For since the creation of the world God's invisible qualities - his eternal power and divine nature - have been clearly seen, being understood from what has been made, so that men are without excuse" <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 1:20</span>.<br>
Creation reveals the attributes and character of God. This is like a rough sketch of the image of God.<br>
In His Son<br>
"No one has ever seen God, but God the only Son, who is at the Father's side, has made him known" <span class="scripture-reference">John 1:18</span> (see also Col.1:15).<br>
In Jesus, we see the nature of God expressed. He is the perfect image of God - not a rough sketch, but an exact likeness. In Christ, we see everything that God is - His love, His holiness, His wisdom and His power - all in perfect balance.<br>
"For God, who said, 'Let light shine out of darkness,' made his light shine in our hearts to give us the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of<br>
Christ" <span class="scripture-reference">2 Corinthians 4:6</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 1:3</span>).




The Gospel<br>
The Gospel means "the good news." It is not like any religion or philosophy in the world.<br>
Many religions teach people to be good. But the Gospel is different: it focuses through a single person, Jesus. This can be a stumbling block to some people.<br>
"...but we preach Christ crucified: a stumbling block to Jews and foolishness to<br>
Gentiles, but to those whom God has called...Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God" <span class="scripture-reference">1 Corinthians 1:22-24</span>.<br>
To understand how the Gospel works and why it focuses solely through Jesus Christ, we need first to understand the human predicament. To appreciate the good news, we first need to know the bad news.<br>
In the Beginning<br>
When God created the first man and woman, He tailor-made them for His presence. They were totally God-centered, God-conscious and God-dependent. But there was one part of<br>
God's creation that had rebelled against God - an angel that we now know as Satan<br>
(<span class="scripture-reference">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Ezekiel 28:12-19</span>). He tempted Adam and Eve to rebel in the same way he had (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Timothy 3:6</span>).



## Essentials For New Disciples 7


"'You will not surely die,' the serpent said to the woman. 'For God knows that when you eat of it your eyes will be opened, and you will be like God, knowing good and evil'" <span class="scripture-reference">Genesis 3:4-5</span>.<br>
Satan's temptation was not just to eat of forbidden fruit. It was for Adam and Eve to rebel against God's command and set themselves up as the god of their lives. When they sinned, they became self-centered, self-conscious and self-dependent. From that time on, self became the center of everything. The "knowledge of good and evil" - of rights and wrongs - became the basis of human life. Sin had cut mankind off from God.<br>
"...sin entered the world through one man, and death through sin, and in this way death came to all men, because all sinned." Rom.5:12.<br>
All the generations following Adam have inherited Adam's fallen nature (<span class="scripture-reference">2 Timothy 3:2</span>-<br>
5). All have come under the power and dominion of Satan (<span class="scripture-reference">1 John 5:19</span>). Because of sin, we were also under God's judgment (<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 1:18-31</span>). We were "objects of wrath," waiting for the verdict to be passed (<span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 2:1-3</span>). There was no way we could help ourselves because even the best of us fell far short of God's glorious standard (read
<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 3:10-12,23</span>).
"All of us have become like one who is unclean, and all our righteous acts are like filthy rags; we all shrivel up like a leaf, and like the wind our sins sweep us away"
<span class="scripture-reference">Isaiah 64:6</span>.
The Good News<br>
"When we were utterly helpless with no way of escape, Christ came at just the right time and died for us sinners" <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 5:6</span> (TLB).<br>
The good news of the Gospel is that God sent Jesus to take our judgment for us. If there was any other way to save us, God would not have sent His Son to suffer the agony of the<br>
Cross. But there was no other way. We were not only sinners, but powerless sinners - powerless to save ourselves or change ourselves. And so Jesus became for us everything that we could not be. He didn't just save us. He became salvation for us. This is the essence of the Gospel message.<br>
"It is because of him that you are in Christ Jesus, who has become for us wisdom from God - that is, our righteousness, holiness and redemption" 1 Corinthians<br>
1:30.<br>
The Power of the Gospel<br>
"...the gospel...is the power of God for the salvation of everyone who believes"
<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 1:16</span>.
The Gospel is the salvation of God in action. It describes how we enter into God's salvation and how that salvation continues to work in our lives.<br>
"For in the gospel a righteousness from God is revealed, a righteousness that is by faith from first to last, just as it is written: 'The righteous will live by faith'"
<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 1:17</span>.
This verse reveals three things about the Gospel:




## Essentials For New Disciples 8


It is from God<br>
The Gospel reveals a righteousness which is "from God." These two words summarize the whole orientation of the Gospel. It is a God-to-you direction, not the other way around. The Gospel sets the compass-needle of our lives to a "from-God" bearing, not a<br>
"from-me" bearing. The Gospel returns our lives to the orientation that God created us for.<br>
"...yet for us there is but one God, the Father, from whom all things came and for whom we live; and there is but one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom all things came and through whom we live" <span class="scripture-reference">1 Corinthians 8:6</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">2 Corinthians 5:17</span>-<br>
18; <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 11:36</span>).<br>
It is by faith<br>
The Gospel reveals the way we receive this gift of righteousness - "by faith" (Philippians<br>
3:8-9). God has designed the Christian life in such a way that as we release our faith toward God, He releases His power to work in our lives. It is faith that reverses the original rebellion and returns our life to God-centeredness, God-consciousness and God- dependence. This faith sets the pace for the rest of our life in Christ.<br>
"And without faith it is impossible to please God, because anyone who comes to him must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who earnestly seek him"
<span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 11:6</span> (see also 4:2).
It is from first to last<br>
The Gospel embraces the whole Christian life from start to finish. We begin as a<br>
Christian by faith in the saving power of God and we live as a Christian by faith in that same saving power. The Gospel does two things:<br>
• It introduces us to Christ (<span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 1:13</span>) - "from first"<br>
• It establishes us in Christ (<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 16:25</span>) - "to last"<br>
Satan works hard to deceive Christians into thinking that after receiving the Lord, we must try to live the Christian life in our own strength (read <span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 3:1-3</span>). But self- effort puts us back into self-centredness. No, the Christian life is all by faith and all by grace. The Gospel is a "from first to last" experience.<br>
"...All over the world this gospel is bearing fruit and growing, just as it has been doing among you since the day you heard it and understood God's grace in all its truth" <span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 1:6</span>.


JESUS<br>
"In the past God spoke to our forefathers through the prophets at many times and in various ways, but in these last days he has spoken to us by his Son, whom he appointed heir of all things, and through whom he made the universe" <span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>.<br>
Two thousand years ago, Jesus appeared on the scene of history. He was born into the world and grew up just like every other person, but He was no ordinary man. He was called the "Son of God" (<span class="scripture-reference">Luke 1:26-38</span>) and the "Word of God."




## Essentials For New Disciples 9


"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was<br>
God.The Word became flesh and lived for a while among us. We have seen his glory, the glory of the one and only Son, who came from the Father, full of grace and truth" <span class="scripture-reference">John 1:14</span>.<br>
The Coming of Jesus<br>
"For the Son of Man came to seek and to save what was lost" <span class="scripture-reference">Luke 19:10</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 1:13</span>).<br>
Jesus was sent into the world to fulfill the wonderful plan of God for our lives. His mission was:<br>
• To give His life as a ransom for us (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 20:28</span>)<br>
• To destroy Satan's works (<span class="scripture-reference">1 John 3:8</span>)<br>
• To give us eternal life (<span class="scripture-reference">1 John 5:11-12</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">John 3:15-17</span>)<br>
• To give us birth into God's family (<span class="scripture-reference">John 1:12</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">John 3:3</span>)<br>
• To restore fellowship with God (<span class="scripture-reference">2 Corinthians 5:18</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 5:1-2</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">1 John 1:3</span>)<br>
The coming of Jesus changed our lives forever. He was the door through which heaven was opened to us (<span class="scripture-reference">John 10:9</span>).<br>
Jesus Represented God<br>
"The Son is the radiance of God's glory and the exact representation of his being, sustaining all things by his powerful word..." <span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 1:3</span> (see also Colossians<br>
1:15-17).<br>
Jesus is called "the Word of God" (<span class="scripture-reference">John 1:1-4,14</span>). He existed eternally before He took on human nature (<span class="scripture-reference">Philippians 2:6-7</span>). As the Word, He communicated the heart of God to mankind. In Jesus, we see what God the Father is like.<br>
"If you really knew me \[Jesus\], you would know my Father as well. From now on, you do know him and have seen him . Anyone who has seen me has seen the<br>
Father. Believe me when I say that I am in the Father and the Father is in me."
<span class="scripture-reference">John 14:7-11</span> (see also 1:18).
He showed us God's love, holiness, wisdom and power.<br>
Jesus Represented Man<br>
"For what the law was powerless to do in that it was weakened by the sinful nature, God did by sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful man to be a sin offering" <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 8:3</span>.<br>
In Jesus we not only see the perfect image of God, we also see the perfect image of man, as God intended us to be. He didn't just take on human form; He completely identified with life as we know it. He shared totally in our humanity.<br>
"Who, being in very nature God, did not consider equality with God something to be grasped, but made himself nothing, taking the very nature of a servant, being made in human likeness. And being found in appearance as a man, he humbled himself and became obedient to death - even death on a cross!" <span class="scripture-reference">Philippians 2:6-8</span>.<br>
During his life on earth, Jesus experienced all the troubles of life that we face, and so understands how we feel (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 8:17</span>). In this way Jesus became the example for us.



## Essentials For New Disciples 10


In His words, His actions and His character He showed us how to live a life pleasing to<br>
God (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 4:1-2</span>; 2:21-25).<br>
"For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but we have one who has been tempted in every way, just as we are - yet was without sin" <span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 4:15</span>.<br>
Jesus Died For Us<br>
"But we see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels, now crowned with glory and honor because he suffered death, so that by the grace of God he might taste death for everyone" <span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 2:9</span>.<br>
Wicked men took the Lord Jesus and executed him by nailing him to a wooden cross like a common criminal (read <span class="scripture-reference">Mark 15:16-39</span>). He could have saved himself, but he did not for it was through his death on the Cross that God was going to save the world. Jesus died for us!<br>
"He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree, so that we might die to sins and live for righteousness; by his wounds you have been healed" <span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 2:24</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">Isaiah 53:5-6</span>).<br>
Jesus Rose For Us<br>

Repentance<br>
"The Lord is not slow in keeping his promise, as some understand slowness. He is patient with you, not wanting anyone to perish, but everyone to come to repentance" <span class="scripture-reference">2 Peter 3:9</span>.<br>
Repentance is the first step we take in personally receiving the salvation that comes through Jesus Christ. Repentance is the gateway into all the blessings God has prepared for us.<br>
"Repent, then, and turn to God, so that your sins may be wiped out, that times of refreshing may come from the Lord" <span class="scripture-reference">Acts 3:19</span>.<br>
Repentance causes great joy in heaven (<span class="scripture-reference">Luke 15:7</span>). But before we can understand true repentance, we need to understand what repentance is not.<br>
• Not just feeling guilty - feeling guilty about our sin comes before repentance, but is not repentance itself. No one repents unless they first feel guilty about their sin, but not all who feel guilty actually repent (read <span class="scripture-reference">Acts 24:25</span>).<br>
• Not just being sorry for your sin - some people are very sorry because of the consequences of their sin. They are not really sorry for the sin, but for the penalty they receive in being caught (<span class="scripture-reference">2 Corinthians 7:10</span>).<br>
• Not trying to be a good person - many people try in their own strength to become a better person and change their way of life. Any self-effort has a root of self- righteousness in it, which does not acknowledge the need of repentance from sin<br>
(<span class="scripture-reference">Isaiah 64:6</span>).<br>
• Not becoming religious - the Pharisees were extremely religious. They fasted and prayed and had many religious ceremonies, yet never repented (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 3:7-10</span>;<br>
5:20).<br>
• Not just knowing the truth - having an intellectual head-knowledge of truth does not guarantee that the truth has become a living reality in one's life (<span class="scripture-reference">James 2:19</span>-<br>
20; Matt.3:8).<br>
True Repentance<br>
Genuine repentance is the result of the work of the Holy Spirit in our hearts. Several things mark this kind of repentance:<br>
• Being sorry to God for your sin (<span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 51:1-4</span>; 38:8).<br>
• Being truthful about your sin (<span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 32:5</span>).<br>
• Changing focus to the Lord (<span class="scripture-reference">2 Chronicles 7:14</span>).<br>
• Receiving the forgiveness of God (<span class="scripture-reference">1 John 1:9</span>).<br>
• Taking responsibility before God (<span class="scripture-reference">Luke 19:8</span>; Lev.6:1-7).<br>
To repent means "to turn around, to change direction." Repentance means turning from four things:<br>
• Turning from sin (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 3:10</span>; Gal.5:19-21; Eph.5:5).<br>
• Turning from the world (<span class="scripture-reference">1 John 2:15</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">James 4:4</span>).<br>
• Turning from yourself (<span class="scripture-reference">2 Corinthians 5:15</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Luke 14:26</span>).<br>
• Turning from the devil (<span class="scripture-reference">Acts 26:18</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 1:13</span>).<br>
But just turning from these things is not enough. It is what we turn to that makes the difference.

Distinctions between worship and praise.<br>
1\. Definitions:<br>
Praise - to commend, to applaud, to express approval or admiration of, to extol in words or in song, to magnify, shine, boast, rave etc. It is an outward expression.

Worship - The Greek word carries the meaning to kiss, like a dog licking his master’s hand. Also, it means to do reverence to, adore, fawn or crouch, worship etc. It is difficult to get a simple definition of worship because real worship defies definition. It is something to be experienced. It is the result of a divine encounter that involves the feelings that are born out an intimate communion with God. It is a response to a revelation of God.

Praise and worship are so closely related and affect each other so much that it difficult to completely separate them into narrow boxes of definition.

How are we to worship God?<br>
Jesus tells us how to worship in <span class="scripture-reference">John 4:24</span>.<br>

2\. Worship in Truth - Jesus Christ is the truth. Jn 14:6. Jesus intends that we worship is accordance with the truths of God’s word. Jn 17:17, Ps 119. The doctrinal content of our songs determine their real spiritual value.<br>
3\. Worship with our whole heart. <span class="scripture-reference">Ps 111:1</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Ps 9:1</span> It includes our emotions.

Our generation is no different.<br>

Our salvation does not just mean we are rescued from an eternity in hell (the future). Our salvation extends into the here and now.

"For we know that our old self was crucified with him so that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves to sin - because anyone who has died has been freed from sin" <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 6:6-7</span>.

The Bible refers to two ways a Christian can walk - in the Spirit or in the flesh. These two walks are opposed to each other (<span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 5:17-18</span>). These two walks cannot be mixed.<br>
You are either walking in the Spirit or you are walking in the flesh.<br>
Walking in the Flesh<br>
The word "flesh" has the meaning of "self." To be living "in the flesh" involves three areas:<br>
• Carnal mind - the thoughts/motives of the flesh<br>
• Appetites - the cravings/acts of the flesh<br>

The Carnal Mind<br>
"Those who live according to the \[flesh\] have their minds set on what that nature desires; but those who live in accordance with the Spirit have their minds set on what the Spirit desires...the \[flesh\] is hostile to God. It does not submit to God's law, nor can it do so" Rom.8:5,7.<br>
The carnal mind - which is the stronghold of the flesh - is a mind governed by the five senses (what we see, hear, touch, taste and smell). The spiritual mind, however, is governed by what God says about a situation. One example of this is the story of the 12 spies in <span class="scripture-reference">Numbers 13:17-14</span>:11.<br>
The ten spies were governed by a carnal mind. They believed what they saw. They fed their minds with it, and the result was unbelief. They fed their emotions with it, and the result was fear. They fed their wills with it, and the result was rebellion.<br>
Joshua and Caleb saw exactly the same things as the others, but had different reactions because they had a different kind of mind. They believed in God's word. They fed their minds with it, and the result was faith. They fed their emotions with it, and the result was courage. They fed their wills with it, and the result was obedience. This is why the<br>
"renewing of our minds" (Rom.12:2) is such a high priority in God's plan for our lives.<br>

The older brother never left his father, but he was never with his father. He was always trying to get his father's approval by working in the fields like one of the servants. This is a picture of many Christians who have fallen into the trap of the Law.<br>

The Model of Family Life<br>

The Cycle of Selfishness<br>
After their fall, God told Adam and Eve the consequences of their sin. For Eve it would be:<br>
"...your husband...will rule over you" <span class="scripture-reference">Genesis 3:16</span>.<br>

• The wife responds with a spirit of independence<br>

This cycle of self-destruction is part of the curse of sin (read <span class="scripture-reference">Genesis 3:16-19</span>). The end result of this selfish lifestyle is every kind of family problem and broken relationship (<span class="scripture-reference">2 Timothy 3:2</span>-<br>

The Cycle of Love<br>
"Wives, submit to your husbands as to the Lord...Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her...Children, obey your parents in the Lord, for this is right" <span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 5:22,25</span>; 6:1 (read also 5:22-6:4;<br>
Col.3:18-21; <span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 3:7</span>).<br>

• The husband's expression of love is laying down his life.<br>
• The wife's expression of love is submitting.

Scripture. God's will is revealed through two expressions:<br>
• Commands (<span class="scripture-reference">John 14:15,21,23-24</span>).<br>

(Rom.12:2).<br>
"If you remain in me and my words remain in you, ask whatever you wish, and it will be given you." <span class="scripture-reference">John 15:7</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 10:8</span>).<br>
As we spend quality time with Jesus and receive His word planted in our hearts (James<br>
1:21-22), we learn the will of God. The purpose of God centers in Christ (<span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 1:9</span>) and all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden in Him (<span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 2:2-3</span>).<br>
Therefore our relationship with Jesus is the foundation upon which God brings His guidance.


Walking in Grace<br>
"May the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all" <span class="scripture-reference">2 Corinthians 13:14</span>.<br>
Grace is God's "indescribable gift" to the believer (2 Cor.9:14-15). As Christians, we are:<br>
• Saved by grace (<span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 2:1-9</span>; 1:4-8).<br>
• Standing in grace (<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 5:1-2</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Acts 13:43</span>).<br>
• Strengthened in grace (2 Tim.2:1; 2 Cor.12:9; Heb.13:9).<br>
• Taught by grace (<span class="scripture-reference">Titus 2:11-12</span>).<br>
• Energized by grace (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Corinthians 15:10</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Acts 4:33</span>; 6:8).<br>
• Growing in grace (<span class="scripture-reference">2 Peter 3:18</span>).<br>
The Danger of Self-Effort<br>
"You who are trying to be justified by law have been alienated from Christ; you have fallen away from grace" Gal.5:4 (see Heb.12:15).<br>
The Bible says it is possible for a Christian to "fall away from grace". This does not mean that he has lost his salvation; rather, it means that he has separated himself from the effect of God's grace in his life. Paul says that those "who are trying to be justified by law...have fallen from grace."<br>
"What, then, was the purpose of the law? It was added because of transgressions until the Seed to whom the promise referred had come. So the law was put in charge to lead us to Christ that we might be justified by faith. Now that faith has come, we are no longer under the supervision of the law" <span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 3:19, 24-25</span><br>
(read also v.20-23).<br>
The Law of Moses was given to reveal sin. Its purpose was to act like a mirror. When we look into a mirror and see that our face is dirty, we do not pick up the mirror and rub our face with it to try to get clean. Its purpose is simply to show us that we need cleaning, but we go to another source - the water - to be made clean. In the same way, the Law was

added for a short period of time to show mankind their need of salvation, and to point to the One who would make us clean (Rev.1:5; <span class="scripture-reference">Titus 3:5</span>).<br>
"Therefore no one will be declared righteous in his sight by observing the law;<br>
rather, through the law we become conscious of sin" <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 3:20</span>.<br>
The covenant of the Law that God gave to Moses has nothing to do with us (read
<span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 3:15-17</span>). It was simply added to preserve Israel until the coming of the
Messiah. We are part of the covenant that God made with Abraham and with Christ<br>
(<span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 3:7,29</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 4:1-5,16,22-24</span>). If we get caught up again in self-effort and legalism, we are walking in the wrong covenant! (read <span class="scripture-reference">2 Corinthians 3:6-18</span>).<br>
"I do not set aside the grace of God, for if righteousness could be gained through the law, Christ died for nothing!" <span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 2:21</span>.<br>
The Curse of the Law<br>
All who try to live by the Law through self-effort are under an automatic curse (Galatians<br>
3:10-14; <span class="scripture-reference">James 2:10</span>), for under the light of the Law two things are revealed:<br>
• God's righteous standard and judgment (Matt.5:21-48).<br>
• Our inability to reach that standard (Rom.3:23; 7:14-25).<br>
God knew that we could never keep His standard of righteousness, so He sent Jesus to take that curse upon Himself and set us free from the Law's demand of judgment.<br>
"For what the law was powerless to do in that it was weakened by the sinful nature, God did by sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful man to be a sin offering. And so he condemned sin in sinful man, in order that the righteous requirements of the law might be fully met in us." <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 8:3-4</span> (see also
<span class="scripture-reference">Philippians 3:3</span>).
Christians Under Law<br>
"You foolish Galatians!...After beginning with the Spirit, are you now trying to attain your goal by human effort?" <span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 3:1-3</span> (read also 1:6-7).<br>
We are born again by the Spirit of God (<span class="scripture-reference">John 3:3,6</span>). But many Christians then try to live the rest of their lives under law. In other words, they try to live up to God's standard by self-effort. But it doesn't work! When a Christian lives in legalism, his life becomes characterized by constant struggle (<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 7:1-25</span>), self-condemnation (<span class="scripture-reference">James 2:10</span>), a lack of joy (<span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 4:15</span>), alienation from God (<span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 5:4</span>) and condemnation toward others (<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 2:1-3</span>). So terrible is this life under law that God actually took the law and crucified it to the Cross!<br>
"...having canceled the written code, with its regulations, that was against us and that stood opposed to us; he took it away, nailing it to the cross" <span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 2:14</span>.<br>
The Christian life was designed to work by God's grace. It is the Holy Spirit who causes us to grow and become more like Christ (2 Cor.3:17-18). The Bible says we are now dead to the Law (<span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 2:19</span>) and living under grace (<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 6:14</span>).<br>
"But now, by dying to what once bound us, we have been released from the law so that we serve in the new way of the Spirit, and not in the old way of the written code" <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 7:6</span> (read also verse 1-5).

The New Way of the Spirit<br>
"So I say, live by the Spirit, and you will not gratify the desires of the sinful nature" <span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 5:16</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">Philippians 3:8-9</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 8:1-2</span>; Hebrews<br>

The Holy Spirit has been given to empower you to keep God's holy standard. He writes<br>

The "new way of the Spirit" is a life of inner empowerment, not of outward law. 35). This inner love fulfils everything required by the written code. But like everything else with God's grace, this love comes from God not from us. All we have to do is walk in this grace!<br>

Walking in Humility<br>
"Therefore, as God's chosen people, holy and dearly loved, clothe yourselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness and patience" <span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 3:12</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 3:8</span>; 5:5).<br>
Humility is one of the products of the Christian life (<span class="scripture-reference">James 3:13</span>). We are not just solitary<br>
Christians. God has placed us in the Body of Christ (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Corinthians 12:12,27</span>). For the<br>
Body of Christ to function as God intended, each member of the Body needs to express humility (1Cor.12:14-26; Rom.12:3-5).<br>
The Humility of the Cross<br>
"And being found in appearance as a man, he humbled himself and became obedient to death - even death on a cross!" <span class="scripture-reference">Philippians 2:8</span>.<br>
In the days of the early Church, to believe in the Cross meant embracing great humility (1
<span class="scripture-reference">Corinthians 1:18, 23-24</span>). The Cross was a symbol of great shame (<span class="scripture-reference">Galatians 3:13</span>; 5:11;
<span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 12:2</span>). But God has chosen this horrible death as a means to deal with mankind's greatest problem: self.
"For you know that...you were redeemed from the empty way of life handed down to you from your forefathers" <span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 1:18</span>.<br>
When Adam and Eve sinned, they chose the way of self (<span class="scripture-reference">Genesis 3:5-6</span>). Ever since, self has been the root of every sin. When Jesus died on the Cross, God dealt with two things:

Sins<br>
"He is the atoning sacrifice for our sins, and not only for ours but also for the sins of the whole world" <span class="scripture-reference">1 John 2:2</span> (see <span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 2:24</span>; 3:18).<br>
The Cross dealt with our sins so that we could receive the forgiveness of God (Col.2:13-
14) and eternal life (<span class="scripture-reference">1 John 5:11</span>). But that was not the end of the work of the Cross.
There was one more thing that had to be dealt with once and for all.<br>
Self<br>
"For we know that our old self was crucified with him..." Rom.6:6.<br>
Many Christians receive God's forgiveness, but then bring an unresolved self over into the Christian life. This is what causes all the dissensions, competition and problems in the<br>
Church (1 Cor.3:1-3; <span class="scripture-reference">James 3:14-16</span>; 4:1-4). But once we realise that we have been crucified with Christ (Gal.2:20; 6:14) - that our self was dealt with once and for all on the<br>
Cross (Rom.6:10-11) - the grace of God is released in our lives to bring true humility.<br>
"And he died for all, that those who live should no longer live for themselves but for him who died for them and was raised again" <span class="scripture-reference">2 Corinthians 5:15</span> (read also verses 14-17).<br>
Understanding Humility<br>
"Though the Lord is on high, he looks upon the lowly, but the proud he knows from afar" <span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 138:6</span> (see <span class="scripture-reference">Isaiah 57:15</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Proverbs 16:5</span>).<br>
The pride of self-effort and self-righteousness is fiercely opposed by God (Proverbs<br>
8:13). In fact, pride is sin (<span class="scripture-reference">Proverbs 21:4</span>). We need to understand the nature of pride before we can understand the nature of humility (read <span class="scripture-reference">2 Timothy 3:1-5</span>).<br>
False humility<br>
"Do not let anyone who delights in false humility...disqualify you for the prize..."
<span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 2:18</span> (see also verse 23).
False humility is an outward humility only (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 6:16</span>). This false humility is designed for other people to see. Its focus is self. It is just a subtle form of pride.<br>
True humility<br>
"Humble yourselves before the Lord..." <span class="scripture-reference">James 4:10</span>.<br>
True humility is a recognition that I can't do it by myself. I need the grace of God and I need other Christians. The focus of true humility is God, not self. True humility has no hint of self-effort, but admits a total dependence on God. It is the one condition that God requires for grace to continue in our lives.<br>
"...God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble" <span class="scripture-reference">James 4:6</span> (read also
<span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 25:9</span>).
Learning from Jesus<br>
"Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls" <span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 11:29</span>.<br>
Jesus is the great example of humility. If we want to express the kind of humility that pleases God, we must learn from Jesus (Phil.2:5-8). Jesus showed that true humility is not

just speaking humble words. It is acting in humility by serving others - taking up our cross on behalf of others (<span class="scripture-reference">Luke 9:23</span>; Eph.5:1-2). It is not speaking "humbly" about oneself, but it is esteeming others (Phil.2:3). It is not long prayers about our unworthiness. It is a continuing sense of awe at the goodness of God to us.<br>
The Fear of God<br>
"The Lord...will be the sure foundation for your times, a rich store of wisdom and knowledge; the fear of the Lord is the key to this treasure" <span class="scripture-reference">Isaiah 33:5-6</span> (see

The fear of the Lord is called "the beginning of wisdom" (<span class="scripture-reference">Proverbs 9:10</span>). It is also the starting place for humility. This is not a fear that causes us to hide from God, but an awe of His power and holiness which causes us to see our lives in the right perspective<br>
(<span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 12:28-29</span>). A fear of God produces a genuine humility that actually draws us closer to Him (<span class="scripture-reference">James 4:6-8</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 3:8</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 3:10</span>).<br>
"Since we have these promises, dear friends, let us purify ourselves from everything that contaminates body and spirit, perfecting holiness out of reverence for God" <span class="scripture-reference">1 Corinthians 7:1</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">Acts 9:31</span>).<br>
The fear of the Lord is the key to walking in holiness. The fear of the Lord involves five important things:<br>
• A knowledge of God (<span class="scripture-reference">Proverbs 9:10</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 1:15-17</span>).<br>
• A hatred of evil (<span class="scripture-reference">Proverbs 8:13</span>; 16:6).<br>
• A desire not to grieve God (<span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 4:30</span>).<br>
• A gratitude for grace (<span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 130:3-4</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Philippians 2:12</span>).<br>
• A turning to God (<span class="scripture-reference">Hosea 12:6</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 25:12-14</span>).<br>
Jesus had a "Spirit ...of the fear of the Lord" upon Him (<span class="scripture-reference">Isaiah 11:2-5,9</span>). Through the same Spirit, we walk in the same humility before the Lord.<br>
"...This is the one I esteem: he who is humble and contrite in spirit, and trembles at my word" <span class="scripture-reference">Isaiah 66:2</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">Jeremiah 5:22</span>).


Walking in Forgiveness<br>
"As water reflects a face, so a man's heart reflects the man" <span class="scripture-reference">Proverbs 27:19</span>.<br>
The condition of our heart is the key to our life in Christ (<span class="scripture-reference">Proverbs 4:23</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 6:21</span>).<br>
God searches our hearts, revealing areas that will block our relationship with Him (1
<span class="scripture-reference">Samuel 16:7</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 8:27</span>).
Three Gifts of Forgiveness<br>
"I, even I, am he who blots out your transgressions, for my own sake, and remembers your sins no more" <span class="scripture-reference">Isaiah 43:25</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 103:1-3</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Micah 7:19</span>).<br>
The Bible talks about three gifts of forgiveness:<br>
• The forgiveness God gives to us (<span class="scripture-reference">1 John 1:9</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Luke 7:47</span>).<br>
• The forgiveness we give to ourselves (Phil.3:13-14).

• The forgiveness we give to others (<span class="scripture-reference">Luke 17:3-4</span>).<br>
"Bear with each other and forgive whatever grievances you may have against one another. Forgive as the Lord forgave you" <span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 3:13</span> (see also Ephesians<br>
4:32).<br>
These three gifts of forgiveness are important in our lives. We must understand God's gift of forgiveness to us, through the shed blood of Jesus (<span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 1:13-14</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 1:2</span>).<br>
But we must also learn to forgive ourselves - which simply means receiving God's gift of forgiveness and agreeing with it. Now we are obliged to forgive others. If we refuse to forgive others, then unforgiveness will block the daily expression of God's forgiveness in our lives.<br>
"For if you forgive men when they sin against you, your heavenly Father will also forgive you. But if you do not forgive men their sins, your Father will not forgive your sins" <span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 6:14-15</span> (read also 18:21-35).<br>
Stages of Unforgiveness<br>
• Initial hurt - someone says or does something that wounds our hearts (Psalm<br>
109:22).<br>
• Resentment - if we don't deal with it straight away, we begin to harbor resentment. Our hearts dwell on the hurt. We are filled with self-pity and a continuing anger against the person who hurt us (<span class="scripture-reference">Colossians 3:8-10,12-14</span>).<br>
• Retaliation - we get back at that person, usually either by not speaking to them, or by slandering them to others (<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 12:17-20</span>).<br>
• Bitterness - this is where we allow unforgiveness to defile our hearts (Hebrews<br>
12:15; <span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 4:29-32</span>).<br>
Living One Day at a Time<br>
"Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself.<br>
Each day has enough trouble of its own" <span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 6:34</span>.<br>
Many Christians are either a prisoner of the future (through worry) or a prisoner of the past (through guilt and unforgiveness). God wants to set us free so that we can live each day with Him - a day at a time.<br>
"'In your anger do not sin': Do not let the sun go down while you are still angry, and do not give the devil a foothold" <span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 4:26-27</span>.<br>
One simple principle in forgiveness is not to allow feelings of hurt and unforgiveness to enter a second day. Each evening we can get things right with God and with others. One other provision God has made is the communion table. Whenever we partake of the<br>
Lord's Supper together, we are instructed to make sure we are not holding anything against a brother or sister (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Corinthians 11:27-31</span>).<br>
A Continuing Debt<br>
"Let no debt remain outstanding, except the continuing debt to love one another..." <span class="scripture-reference">Romans 13:8</span>.<br>
When Jesus died for you, he took the judgment you deserved. You now owe Him a massive debt which you have no ability to repay. But God doesn't require you to repay it

-except in one way: God has transferred this debt to Him onto others around us. We are now indebted to everyone. The only way we repay this continuing debt is by forgiving one another. When we consider how great our debt is to the Lord, is this too big to ask?<br>
We now express our love to God by loving others. We cannot be close to God while harboring hatred and unforgiveness.<br>
"If anyone says, 'I love God,' yet hates his brother, he is a liar. For anyone who does not love his brother, whom he has seen, cannot love God, whom he has not seen" <span class="scripture-reference">1 John 4:20</span>.<br>
When we forgive others, we acknowledge several things:<br>

• Our love for God (<span class="scripture-reference">Luke 7:47</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">1 John 4:20</span>).<br>
• Our trust in God's justice (<span class="scripture-reference">2 Thessalonians 1:3-7</span>).<br>
• Our faith in God's plan for us (<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 8:28</span>).<br>
• Our readiness to suffer abuse (Matt.5:11-12).<br>

• Our secure position in Christ (Col.1:1-4).<br>
If I forgive, will the hurt I feel go away? Probably not immediately. But forgiveness has nothing to do with feelings. Forgiveness is a decision. Forgetting is a process. If we decide not to forgive, the hurt is like an open, festering wound, which is very difficult to heal. When we make the decision to forgive, the hurt is cleaned up, and the healing process is then quick.<br>
Healing the Heart<br>
"He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds" Psa.147:3.<br>
God is able to heal every heart. In fact, this is part of the Gospel message (<span class="scripture-reference">Isaiah 61:1</span>).<br>
But more than that, God wants to use us to minister that healing to others. Only when we have experienced both the personal forgiveness of God and the grace to forgive others can we be used to minister healing to others.<br>
"Praise be to the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of compassion and the God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our troubles, so that we can comfort those in any trouble with the comfort we ourselves have received from God" <span class="scripture-reference">2 Corinthians 1:3-4</span>.

"You have made known to me the path of life; you will fill me with joy in your presence, with eternal pleasures at your right hand" <span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 16:11</span> (see also118:24).<br>
Joy is part of the inheritance of the Christian, the evidence of the work of God's Spirit in his life. Nehemiah said that "the joy of the Lord is your strength" (<span class="scripture-reference">Nehemiah 8:10</span>). This joy is part of the abundant life that Jesus came to give us.

Abundant Life<br>
"The thief comes only to steal and kill and destroy; I have come that they may have life, and have it to the full" <span class="scripture-reference">John 10:10</span>.<br>
Jesus came to give us a new life that was both eternal (in duration) and abundant (in expression). Satan, however, is a thief of that abundant life. Christian joy is not a surface joy. It is not a mask that we put on for others. It is not just froth and bubble, but a deep joy that comes from God's Spirit. But this joy often has a backdrop of repentance and weeping (<span class="scripture-reference">James 4:8</span>). Even the apostle Paul said that he was<br>
"sorrowful, yet always rejoicing" (2 Cor.6:10). God's desire is to turn our sorrow into rejoicing.<br>
"Those who sow in tears will reap with songs of joy. He who goes out weeping, carrying seed to sow, will return with songs of joy, carrying sheaves with him"<br>

The Christian has every reason to rejoice. We rejoice because God is on the Throne (1<br>
Chron.16:31), because we have peace with God (Rom.5:1-2,11), because we have a share in Christ's inheritance (Col.1:11-12), because our names are written in heaven (Luke<br>
10:20), because we have been released from Satan's bondage (<span class="scripture-reference">Acts 8:5-8</span>) and because we have received such a great salvation (<span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 13:5</span>; 105:43; Isa.51:3,11; 55:12). "Those living far away fear your wonders; where morning dawns and evening fades you call forth songs of joy." <span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 65:8</span> (see also 46:4; 47:1,5; 66:1-2; 95:1-<br>
2).

"I have told you this so that my joy may be in you and that your joy may be complete" <span class="scripture-reference">John 15:11</span>.<br>

Christ (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Thessalonians 1:6</span>). But many Christians wonder where that first joy goes. In fact, it didn't go anywhere. What is needed is a continuing walk of obedience. You can't live off the joy of old obedience; it is a daily walk with the Lord and a daily experience of joy.<br>
"...I say these things while I am still in the world, so that they may have the full measure of my joy within them" <span class="scripture-reference">John 17:13</span>.<br>
God has joy (<span class="scripture-reference">Zephaniah 3:17</span>). Jesus said that when we obey His commands - not out of duty, but because we love Him - then not only will our joy be complete, but His joy will also fill us. We get double joy - our joy in obeying the Lord and His joy on top of that!<br>

Because our joy comes from the Holy Spirit and not from the circumstances, Christians have a joy which is unconquerable.<br>

The Nature of God<br>

"Righteousness and justice are the foundation of your throne; love and faithfulness go before you" <span class="scripture-reference">Psalm 89:14</span> (see also 85:10-11; <span class="scripture-reference">Jeremiah 9:23-24</span>).<br>

What Balance is Not<br>
Balance is not compromise. Compromise is based not on a desire to please God, but to please and accommodate man. In contrast to compromise, God's principles always stand firm (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 1:24-25</span>). For this reason God hates:<br>
Lukewarmness<br>
"I know your deeds, that you are neither cold nor hot. I wish you were either one or the other! So, because you are lukewarm - neither hot nor cold - I am about to spit you out of my mouth" Rev.3:15-16.<br>
Lukewarmness is one of the worst forms of compromise, for it affects our relationship with God. It is a rejection of God's love that brings more hurt to His heart than even fully turning one's back on Him. John states uncompromisingly:<br>
"Do not love the world or anything in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him" <span class="scripture-reference">1 John 2:15</span> (see also <span class="scripture-reference">2 Corinthians 6:14-18</span>).<br>

"But when he asks, he must believe and not doubt, because he who doubts is like a wave of the sea, blown and tossed by the wind. Double-mindedness is a hindrance to spiritual growth. God does not want us to constantly swing from one direction to another. He wants our eyes to be fixed firmly upon Himself.<br>
"Let us fix our eyes on Jesus, the author and perfecter of our faith..." Hebrews<br>
12:2.<br>
Jesus declared, "I am the truth" (<span class="scripture-reference">John 14:6</span>). Just as our physical body needs a focal point to keep its balance, so Jesus is the focal point for our spiritual balance. He is the focus of all truth in God's word (<span class="scripture-reference">John 5:39</span>). We are walking in balance only when we have the<br>
Lord Jesus as our focal point.<br>

God desires His people to live with a great sense of purpose for their lives. And if you address as Father the One who impartially judges according to each man’s work, conduct yourselves in fear during the time of your stay upon earth; knowing that you were not redeemed with perishable things like silver or gold from your futile way of life inherited from your forefathers, but with precious blood, as of a lamb unblemished and spotless, the blood of Christ. <span class="scripture-reference">1 Peter 1:17-19</span>
