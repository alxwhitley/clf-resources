## Slide 4

Saving & Investing

Saving: Setting aside money for short-term goals or emergencies, typically in a low-risk account.

Investing: Using money to purchase assets (stocks, bonds, real estate, etc.) with the expectation of long-term growth and returns.

## Slide 5

Saving provides security and liquidity for short-term needs.

Investing helps grow wealth over time and beats inflation.

Both work together to achieve financial goals (e.g., emergency fund first, then investments for long-term growth).<br>

## Slide 13

Types of Accounts<br>
Step 1: Estimate Annual Retirement Expenses<br>
Example: You expect to spend $50,000 per year in retirement.<br>
Step 2: Use the 25x Rule (for 4% Withdrawal Rate)<br>
Multiply annual expenses by 25 to estimate total savings needed.<br>
$50,000 × 25 = $1.25 million needed for retirement.<br>
Step 3: Adjust for Inflation & Lifestyle<br>
Consider inflation (typically 2-3% per year).<br>
Adjust for additional income sources (Social Security, pension, rental income).<br>

## Slide 18

**Table (review layout):**<br>
Component | Definition | Why It Matters<br>
NAV (Net Asset Value) | Price per share of the mutual fund. | Determines the fund’s value per share at the end of each trading day.<br>
Rating | A score (e.g., from Morningstar) based on past performance and risk. | Helps investors evaluate the fund's quality and reliability.<br>
Risk Category | Classification of the fund’s risk level (low, moderate, high). | Guides investors in choosing a fund based on their risk tolerance.<br>
Performance/Average Annual Returns | Historical returns over different time frames (1-year, 5-year, 10-year). | Helps compare funds and set realistic expectations.<br>
Fund Inception | The date the mutual fund was created. | Older funds with strong performance may indicate reliability.<br>
Expense Ratio | The percentage of assets deducted for management fees. | Lower expense ratios mean lower costs for investors.<br>
Dividends | Earnings distributed to shareholders periodically. | Provides additional income from the investment.<br>
Capital Gains | Profits when fund assets are sold for more than their purchase price. | Impacts taxes and overall returns.
