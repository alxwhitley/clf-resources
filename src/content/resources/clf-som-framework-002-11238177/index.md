CHRISTIAN LIFE FELLOWSHIP<br>
SCHOOL OF MINISTRY<br>
Overview/Program Design

The Christian Life Fellowship School of Ministry (CLF SoM) is an expansion of discipleship within the local church, specifically designed to empower believers as workmen who study to show themselves approved (<span class="scripture-reference">1 Timothy 2:15</span>), to perfect and equip saints for the work of the ministry (<span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 4:12</span>), and to accelerate the growth/training of apostles, prophets, evangelists, and teachers (<span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 4:11</span>).

CLF SoM provides two tracks of study annually, the first for growing disciples and the latter for emerging leaders:

Logos (Track 1) Logos (T1) is a foundational track of study for growing disciples of Jesus<br>
Christ who are interested in deepening their understanding and application of the Bible; instructional modules include biblical survey courses and series of topical study.

Launch (Track 2) Launch (T2) is a track of study, discipleship, and activation for \[emerging leaders and mature\] disciples of Jesus Christ who are interested in developing ministry gifts and launching kingdom initiatives; instructional modules include topical review of the Church, the Kingdom, and dynamics of ministry in each sphere respectively (e.g., offices, calling, administrations, gifting, etc.).

Curriculum Path

Tracks – Modules – Courses – Sessions – Lesson Plan – Resources/Assignments

OR

 Track – outlined and focused progression of study and course completion<br>
1\. Module – units of study, includes 2-4 courses that can be completed in 1-3 months a. Courses – unit of instruction, provided over 1-4 sessions i. Sessions/Classes – scheduled instruction and learning time for each course<br>
1\. Lesson Plan – crafted instructional plan for each course/session a. Resources – identified print or digital materials to facilitate learning b. Reflective Assignment(s) – pre-assigned or take-home assignments designed for students to demonstrate effective learning (i.e., analyze, evaluate, or create something based on key understanding).

This format allows for tracks to be repeated annually, circularly, synchronously, or asynchronously dependent upon need/number of course participants and/or creation of additional tracks for training/discipleship.

Instructors should be assigned at the course level to write course objectives, create lesson plans;<br>
a teaching team (1-3 teachers) could be assigned to develop and/or teach courses taught over 2-4 sessions (this dynamic can also be utilized as a practicum for Launch (T2) participants to work in conjunction with church leadership or established teacher facilitating a Logos (T1) course).

Sample Lesson Plan<br>
Logos (T1) – Old Testament Survey 4 Sessions

Course Description: Truncated survey of the 39 books of the Old Testament (OT); provides specific overview of the 5 major sections: Pentateuch, History, Poetry, Major Prophets, and Minor Prophets; emphasis on historical narrative and overarching, inspired themes.<br>
Learning Outcomes:

1\. Students will ANALYZE & DIAGRAM the coherency of the Old Testament as a historical, yet inspired narrative.<br>
2\. Students will IDENTIFY/INTERPRET the Christological purpose (revelation of Jesus<br>
Christ) for each of the five sections of the Old Testament.<br>
3\. Students will SUMMARIZE the historical and doctrinal significance (3 points) of the Old<br>
Testament in 1-3 paragraphs.<br>
Timeframe: 70 minutes per session (4 hours, 40 minutes total)

## Instructional Plan (Session 1):

 Introduction/Hook 20 min<br>
 Show Bible Project video: “Overview; TaNAK/Old Testament” (YouTube/RNM)<br>
 Distribute OT Reflection Handout / ask students to complete the first entry / explain ongoing Reflective Learning Assignment.<br>
 Compare TaNAK groupings and canonized categories as segway to instruction<br>
 Instruction & Scaffolding 35 min<br>
 Guided lecture through comparison chart of book arrangements (PPT); distribute blank chart and clozed notes for students to fill in during lecture. Identify groupings of OT books, individual authors, and relevance of historical context.<br>
 Independent Practice 10 min<br>
 Students will conduct an internet search for “OT books organized charts” (or review<br>
4-6 arrangements distributed as handouts); compare and evaluate groupings of books found.<br>
 Students will download/access RightNow Media app—explore OT survey Bible studies, select one to review.

Sample Reflective Learning Assignment

Old Testament Survey: Journey through the Bible – Reflective Learning Assignment<br>
Explanation: Bible survey courses are keen opportunities to study the Bible in a pervasive manner, which often includes required reading of every book. The obvious challenge for students, however, is in finding the time, sustained focus, and mental endurance to do so—especially if other priorities and commitments prevail or if reading is not a strength. This assignment leverages time and attention (and side steps reading as a requirement), but still provides the opportunity for a complete review of all 39 Old Testament books in manageable and meaningful chunks.<br>
Directions: Each day, review a short video for one book of the Old Testament from The Bible<br>
Project (use the The Bible Project App or YouTube). Next, reflectively consider the presentation and write down any 1-3 notable insights/connections, new information, fresh revelation, or confirmed truth that stands out to you. If you do this daily, by the last course session, you will have reviewed and thoughtfully reflected upon the entire Old Testament.
