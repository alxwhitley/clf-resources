## Slide 3

What is Credit?<br>
Definition:<br>
Credit is the ability to borrow money or access goods and services with the promise to pay later.<br>
It is based on the trust that payment will be made in the future.<br>
Credit allows consumers to purchase items now and pay for them over time.<br>
Credit can come in various forms such as credit cards, loans, and mortgages.

## Slide 4

Facts & Statistics about Credit in the U.S.<br>
80% of Americans have some form of credit.<br>
The average American credit score is 711.<br>
As of 2023, the total U.S. credit card debt reached over $1 trillion.<br>
Approximately 30% of Americans have a credit score below 600, which is considered "bad credit."<br>
1 in 5 consumers have at least one error on their credit report.<br>
66% of credit report disputes result in successful resolutions, meaning errors are corrected.

Why Is Credit important?<br>
Access to Financial Opportunities: Credit allows access to loans, mortgages, and financing options.<br>
Build Trustworthiness: A good credit score shows lenders that you are a responsible borrower.<br>
Interest Rates: Good credit scores lead to lower interest rates, saving money on loans.<br>
Emergency Situations: Having credit can help during unexpected financial emergencies.

## Slide 8

Payment history (35%)<br>
Debt Validation Letter<br>
Requests proof that a debt is valid and that they have the right to collect it. Under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA), creditors must provide verification of the debt.<br>
Method of Verification Letter<br>
Requests the method a creditor used to verify a debt reported on your credit report. It can be used when the creditor has not provided sufficient evidence or verification of the debt.<br>
Violation of FCRA Rights Letter<br>
Sent when a consumer believes their rights under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) have been violated. For example, if a credit bureau fails to investigate a dispute or fails to correct inaccurate information.<br>
Intent to Litigate Letter<br>
A formal notice to a creditor, collection agency, or credit bureau stating your intention to take legal action if the disputed debt is not resolved or if violations of your rights are not corrected.

## Slide 12

Building Credit<br>
📌 Secured Credit Cards<br>
Requires a cash deposit as collateral (usually your credit limit).<br>
Works like a regular credit card, helping to build credit with responsible use.<br>
Payments are reported to credit bureaus, boosting credit score over time.<br>
Ideal for those with no credit or bad credit.<br>
📌 Credit Builder Loans<br>
A loan where the borrowed amount is held in a bank account until fully paid off.<br>
Payments are reported to credit bureaus, establishing positive credit history.<br>
No upfront money needed—good for first-time borrowers.<br>
At the end of the loan term, you receive the saved-up money.

## Slide 13

How to Build Credit<br>
Make all payments on time.<br>
Keep your credit card or line of credit balances (utilization) low<br>
Avoid opening too many new accounts at once<br>
Keep accounts open for as long as possible<br>
Check each of your credit reports often for errors and discrepancies.<br>
Avoid using credit cards as an emergency fund<br>
Pay your balance down to zero monthly
