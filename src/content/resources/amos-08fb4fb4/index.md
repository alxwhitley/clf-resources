### Amos - (The book of Amos) – Burden Bearer

Author: Amos himself

## Amos The Man

Amos lived in a small town called Tekoa about 6-10 miles from Bethlehem.

Amos was a Sheepherder (7:14). And a collector of figs

Amos prophesied the same time as Hosea

Amos prophesied during King Uzziah’s Reign in Judah, and the latter part of king Jeroboam 2 reign approximately 60-80 years before the captivity of Israel.

Amos prophesied mostly from Bethel during his life. The center of worship in Israel + Samaria

Amos was very well acquainted with the world of his day

He names 38 ancient towns or districts in the ancient near east his writings

He understood the history of the surrounding nations as well as Israel & Judah’s

Most Scholars believe the ministry of Amos was very short. Possibly only up to two years in length

After a short time he was kicked out of the Northern Kingdom and sent back to the southern kingdom, where he wrote chapters 8+9 of Amos.

Because of Amos being originally from the Southern Kingdom of Judah, his prophecies were rather hard at times on the Northern Kingdom for their sins against Jehovah.

The people of Israel were highly religious but corrupt.

They would pack out the temples of Bethel and Gilgal and Dan, entering into the Golden Calf worship of Jeroboam, speak of the day of the lord, yet they lived very immoral.

## Amos’ Message:

## Amos condemns Israel for taking advantage of Jehovah’s Favor turning toward sin,

He pronounces the harsh judgment of Jehovah upon them for their sin is worse than the pagan nations. For they had walked with God and had forsaken him were the surrounding nations had never known God.

Amos’ prophecies were – The message of Judgment and restoration to follow.

Through Amos & Hosea’s writing we can see the clear condition and prosperity of Israel. Israel was so prospering under the rule of Jeroboam that it increased the materialism & greed of the nation.

The rich were strongly inflicting the poor.

Amos’s messages contain key phrases – Hear this word – Woe- yet you have not returned to me!

(3:1, 4:1, 5:1) – (5:18, 6:1) – Chapter 4
