Dealing with the foolish

Helping care for and pastor people can sometimes be a confusing task. We can hear teaching and read books about how to do it but in the real life world of the church we rarely come across “cookie cutter molds” people or situations. Every situation and person is unique and it can sometimes cause us to feel unsure of what direction to take.

This is especially true in dealing with difficult people. With difficult people we can easily get somewhat lost in knowing what direction to take. That is why we must stick close to our compass, the word of God.

As you know a compass is an interesting device. It existed long before GPS systems. No matter where you stand or how lost you are, a compass will always point toward the north pole so you can find your direction again.

God’s word works like a compass. In the midst of helping in difficult circumstances we can feel disoriented as to what to do. God’s word will always point us in His direction.

One of the areas where we must keep His compass in view is in dealing with fools. As any leader knows we have to deal with the foolishness in the lives of people. The Bible has much to say about dealing with fools. Let’s look at the Biblical compass regarding what fools are and how to deal with them.

<span class="scripture-reference">Proverbs 9:7-10</span> He who corrects a scoffer gets dishonor for himself, and he who reproves a wicked man gets insults for himself. Do not reprove a scorner, lets he hate you, reprove a wise man, and he will love you. Give instruction to a wise man, and he will be still wiser, teach a righteous man, and he will increase his learning. The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, and the knowledge of the Holy One is understanding.

## The Classic Biblical Fool

The root cause of the type of personality called the fool is a resistance to being educated. It is a self-imposed problem. The extent to which a man is a fool is a direct result of the extent to which he tries to live without acquiring knowledge or developing his mind, whether in regard to any one thing or as a general lifestyle. Those who most fully embrace this attitude are classic Biblical fools.

From the first chapter of Proverbs, we find that fools blindly reject all knowledge, advice, and counsel. Indeed, they hate knowledge and reproof. They do not choose the fear of the Lord, but instead they turn away from wisdom, and remain complacent in ignorance. Notice their resistance to learning.

…fools despise wisdom and instruction (<span class="scripture-reference">Proverbs 1:7</span>; KJV).

The Biblical definition of a fool is one who hates God’s wisdom and instruction and tends to resist it, especially as it comes through other people.

How does God direct us in dealing with fools?

The Word gives us insight in dealing with fools.

1\. We are warned against much involvement with fools and are instructed to avoid them. Otherwise we are to respond with disapproval.

Pro 13:20 NASB He who walks with wise men will be wise, But the companion of fools will suffer harm.

Pro 14:7 NASB Leave the presence of a fool, Or you will not discern words of knowledge.

Pro 23:9 NASB Do not speak in the hearing of a fool, For he will despise the wisdom of your words.

Pro 26:4-5 NASB Do not answer a fool according to his folly, Or you will also be like him. 5 Answer a fool as his folly deserves, That he not be wise in his own eyes.

2\. We are advised against either praising them or giving them responsibility.

If you do this you will suffer.

Pro 26:1 NASB Like snow in summer and like rain in harvest, So honor is not fitting for a fool.

Pro 26:6 NASB He cuts off his own feet and drinks violence Who sends a message by the hand of a fool.

Pro 26:8 NASB Like one who binds a stone in a sling, So is he who gives honor to a fool.

Pro 26:10 NASB Like an archer who wounds everyone, So is he who hires a fool or who hires those who pass by.

3\. Those in authority, who are required to maintain order, have the responsibility to deal with fools firmly.

Pro 7:22 NASB Suddenly he follows her As an ox goes to the slaughter, Or as one in fetters to the discipline of a fool,

Pro 10:13 NASB On the lips of the discerning, wisdom is found, But a rod is for the back of him who lacks understanding.

Pro 19:29 NASB Judgments are prepared for scoffers, And blows for the back of fools.

Pro 26:3 NASB A whip is for the horse, a bridle for the donkey, And a rod for the back of fools.

4\. But no matter how much fools may be corrected or punished, the ultimate cure for this problem does not come from without.

Pro 17:10 NASB A rebuke goes deeper into one who has understanding Than a hundred blows into a fool.

Pro 27:22 NASB Though you pound a fool in a mortar with a pestle along with crushed grain, Yet his foolishness will not depart from him.

To sum it up, fools are a problem for God’s people. We must reach to God on their behalf that they would see and turn from their foolishness to God’s wisdom. We all have become foolish at one time or another, but for those who are persistent in their foolishness it is best to avoid them.

If we are in responsibility and people’s foolishness causes destruction to others (they get caught up in their foolishness) we have a duty to eliminate their influence. Whatever we do we must be led by the Spirit of God as we take action.

What about offending people.

Jesus was offensive. Most religious people were offended by him. The proof for John the Baptist that Jesus was the Christ was that the blind see, the lame walk and the majority are "offended" by Him (Mat. 11:2-19). As Jesus said, "Blessed is he who is not offended because of Me" (Mat. 11:6; <span class="scripture-reference">Luke 7:23</span>). In , Jesus did not plead with his neighbors to understand Him when "they were offended at Him" (Mat. 13:57; <span class="scripture-reference">Mark 6:3</span>). He offended unbelievers. So be it (cf. <span class="scripture-reference">Luke 14:3-4</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">John 5:8-16</span>). "Shake off the dust from your feet" (Mat. 10:14). But today Christ’s own approach is no longer considered a Christian attitude.

Jesus’ apostles asked Him, "Do you know that the Pharisees were offended when they heard \[Your\] saying?" (Mat. 15:12). Today’s Christians would respond, "Oh no. Quickly, apologize! Ask for forgiveness! Tell them we are sorry. We never meant to offend. There must be a misunderstanding." How did Jesus respond? He said to ignore the complaints of the unbelievers: "Let them alone. They’re blind," (Mat. 15:14). Today, many Christians would condemn Christ’s attitude as unloving.
