ACTS (Part I: Chapters 1-12)

Second volume of a 2 part treatise written by Luke.

Written to Theopholis about “all that Jesus began to do and teach”

Frequent use of medical terms

Luke answered the Macedonian call with Paul, was in charge of the work at Philippi for about six years, and later was with Paul in Rome during the time of Paul’s house arrest. It was probably during this last period that the book was written.

Date: A.D. 61

Likely written in Rome or Caesarea and Rome.

## Theme and Purpose:

historical link between the Gospels and the Epistles.

Begins with Christ’s Ascension and continues to the period of the New Testament Epistles. In it we have the continuation of the ministry of Jesus Christ through the Holy Spirit at work in the apostles who went forth preaching and establishing the church, the body of Christ

Provides an account of the life of Paul and gives us the historical occasion for his letters. In the process, Acts recounts the first 30 years of the life of the church.

Progress of the kingdom message from Jews to Gentiles, and from Jerusalem to Rome

Historical Motive: History shown as Holy Spirit carried on the work of Jesus and that work is seen through the lives of the apostles. Provides about 30 years of history of the early church.

Defense Motive: In the midst of increasing hostility toward Christianity, the gospel was presented to many government officials.

Biographical Motive: Luke divides Acts into 2 sections; 1-12 the history of Peter’s work and 13-28 the work of Paul to the Gentiles.

## Key Word:

The key concept for Acts would be the growth of the Church in all the world.

Two key words are “witness” or “witnesses,” and “the Holy Spirit.”

## Key Chapters:

Since the accomplishment of the church’s global mission of worldwide outreach is dependent on the coming of the Holy Spirit, chapter 2 is naturally the key chapter. This chapter records the fulfillment of 1:8 on the Day of Pentecost when the Holy Spirit came and began His ministry of baptizing believers into Christ’s body, the church (cf. 1:5; 11:15-16 with <span class="scripture-reference">1 Cor. 12:13</span>), began indwelling all believers and empowering them to be witnesses of the Savior.

## Key People:

Key people include: Peter, Stephen, Philip, James, Barnabas and Paul.

## Christ as Seen in Acts:

he resurrected Savior is the central theme of the sermons and defenses in Acts. The Old Testament Scriptures, the historical resurrection, the apostolic testimony, and the convicting power of the Holy Spirit all bear witness that Jesus is both Lord and Christ (see Peter’s sermons in 2:22-36; 20:34-43). “To Him all the prophets witness that, through His name, whoever believes in Him will receive remission of sins” (10:43). “Nor is there salvation in any other, for there is no other name under heaven given among men by which we must be saved” (4:12).35
