“Whosoever will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me. For whosoever will save his life shall lose it: but whosoever shall lose his life for my sake and the Gospel’s, the same shall save it.” <span class="scripture-reference">Mark 8:34-35</span> (KJV)

What is Jesus’ command concerning Discipleship

When Jesus commissioned His disciples after the resurrection, He gave them a fourfold charge:

To Evangelize. “Go ye into all the world , and preach the gospel to every creature” (<span class="scripture-reference">Mark 16:15</span>)

To Make Disciples. “Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit: teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you.” (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 28:19-20</span>)

To Be Witnesses. “But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me…” (<span class="scripture-reference">Acts 1:8</span>)

To Feed Others. “Jesus saith unto him, Feed my sheep.” (<span class="scripture-reference">John 21:17</span>)

These Four things must be experienced in this order in the life of every believer.

What is a Disciple?

The word “disciple” is used over 250 times in the New Testament

## and simply means:

A taught or trained one

A learner or pupil

One who accepts or receives instruction or doctrines

from another

A disciplined one.

The word “discipline”, which is closely related, means:

Training that develops self-control or character.

Submission to control.

Orderly conduct.

Treatment that corrects or adjusts.

What does it mean to be a disciple of Jesus Christ?

It means that we are willing to treat Him as our Lord and Master (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 10:24</span>).

It means that we are willing to be taught and instructed in His ways (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 5:1-2</span>).

It means that we must be willing to respond to His commands (<span class="scripture-reference">Luke 6:46</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 7:21</span>).

It means that we must be willing to be corrected and adjusted by the Word of the Lord (<span class="scripture-reference">Proverbs 3:11-12</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Hebrews 12:5-13</span>).

What does it cost to be a disciple of Jesus Christ?

We are clearly instructed in the Word of God that before we begin building, we should count the cost (<span class="scripture-reference">Luke 14:25-33</span>). Salvation is a free gift from God. We can do nothing to earn it or deserve it (<span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>), but if we are to respond to that call of discipleship, it will cost us everything that we have (<span class="scripture-reference">Luke 5:11</span>).

“He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me: and he that loveth son or daughter more than me is not worthy of me. And he that taketh not his cross, and followeth after me, is not worthy of me.” (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 10:37-38</span>).

This means that everything in our life has to take second place to our relationship to the Lord, including:

Our home (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 8:19, 20</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Luke 9:58</span>).

Our friends and relatives (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 10:37-38</span>).

Our companions (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 4:8-22</span>).

Our possessions (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 19:16-22</span>).

This does not mean that the Lord will demand these things of us, but it does mean that we would be willing to offer them up if He did (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 16:24-26</span>).

What is the reward of the life of Discipleship?

The only reason we can become such a disciple to the Lord Jesus Christ is because He only has our best interest in mind. The Lord never asks us to give something up unless He plans to give us something better in return.

“And Jesus answered and said, Verily I say unto you, There is no man that hath left house, or brethren, or sisters, or father, or mother, or wife, or children, or lands, for my sake, and the gospel’s, but he shall receive a hundredfold now in this time, houses, and brethren, and sisters, and mothers, and children, and lands, with persecutions; and in the world to come eternal life.”
<span class="scripture-reference">Mark 10:20-30</span>

“I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly.” <span class="scripture-reference">John 10:10</span>

What is the goal of the life of Discipleship?

There are several goals that will be achieved for those who become disciples:

To be a strong, mature Christian able to weather the storms of life (<span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 4:13-14</span>).

To be a true reflection of Christ to the world (<span class="scripture-reference">Ephesians 4:13</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Acts 11:26</span>).

To be the kind of Christian that others can follow and pattern their lives after (<span class="scripture-reference">I Corinthians 11:1</span>; <span class="scripture-reference">Titus 2:7-8</span>).

To be the kind of Christian that God can use to minister to others (<span class="scripture-reference">John 15:8, 12-17</span>).

To receive ultimately from the Lord the commendation of a good and faithful servant (<span class="scripture-reference">Matthew 25:21</span>).

“My brothers and sisters, God called you and chose you to be his. Do your best to live in a way that shows you really are God’s called and chosen people. If you do all this, you will never fall. 11 And you will be given a very great welcome into the kingdom of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, a kingdom that never ends.
<span class="scripture-reference">II Peter 1:10-11</span>

## Conclusion:

The life of discipleship is really not an option for those who want to reach the above goals in their life. Unfortunately, many believers live a life far below God’s standard for them and, hence, never fully experience the great rewards and blessings that come from a life of total surrender to the King of Kings and Lord of Lords. As believers, we must be willing to accept the challenge of discipleship if we are to prove “what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect will of God” (<span class="scripture-reference">Romans 12:1-2</span>).
